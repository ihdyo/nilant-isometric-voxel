## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Tawny-gold coat covered in round black spots, a deep chest, narrow waist, small head with distinct black tear-streaks from eyes to mouth, and semi-retractable claws that grip the ground like cleats [1].

### Size and weight
Head-and-body length 1.1–1.5 m, tail 60–80 cm, shoulder height 67–94 cm, and weight 21–72 kg [1].

### Lifespan
10–12 years in the wild; up to 17 years in captivity [1].

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Diurnal hunter targeting medium-sized antelopes such as Thomson's gazelles, impalas and springboks, plus hares and ground birds [1].

### Habitat
Open savannas, dry grasslands, scrublands and semi-deserts across eastern and southern Africa, with a critically small population in central Iran [2].

### Geographic distribution and native regions
Fragmented populations in eastern and southern Africa (Namibia, Botswana, Kenya, Tanzania) and central Iran [2].

### Habits and behaviour
Unlike most big cats, hunts primarily by day using keen vision and bursts of speed up to 100–120 km/h over short distances [1, 3].

### Social structure
Females are solitary except with dependent cubs; adult males often form lifelong sibling coalitions [1].

### Reproduction
Gestation lasts 90–98 days, producing 3–5 cubs that stay with their mother for 18–24 months [1].

### Predators and threats
Habitat loss and fragmentation, prey depletion, human-wildlife conflict and illegal pet trade [2].

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Enlarged nostrils, heart and lungs support extreme oxygen intake during sprints, while a flexible spine and long rudder-like tail enable sharp turns [1, 3].

### Ecological role and importance
Coursing predator of open grasslands that shapes herbivore vigilance and distribution [1].

### Interesting facts
- The fastest land mammal on Earth, accelerating from 0 to 100 km/h in about three seconds. [3]
- Black tear marks running down its face deflect harsh savanna glare and help keep prey in focus. [1]

### Uncertain or variable information
Top sprint speeds of 100–120 km/h reflect peer-reviewed GPS/accelerometer measurements (~93 km/h in wild hunts) alongside classic physiological estimates [1, 3].
