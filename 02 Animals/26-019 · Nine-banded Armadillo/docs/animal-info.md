## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
A shell of bony plates under leathery skin, about 16 % of its weight; usually nine bands (8–11); hairless ears; a paler, unarmoured belly; it cannot roll into a perfect ball [1].

### Size and weight
Body about 62–80 cm, tail about 30 cm with 12–15 rings; 3.6–7.7 kg [1].

### Lifespan
7 to more than 20 years in the wild, over 23 in captivity [1].

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Mostly animals (over 90 %): beetles above all, termites, ants, millipedes, worms, small reptiles; smells food up to 20 cm underground [1].

### Habitat
Forest, savanna, shrubland and grassland, up to 2,300 m [2].

### Geographic distribution and native regions
South America from Colombia and Venezuela to Argentina and Uruguay [2].

### Habits and behaviour
Digs burrows up to 5 m long; holds its breath up to 6 minutes and swallows air to float; jumps straight up when startled; mostly nocturnal and solitary [1].

### Social structure
Mostly solitary [1].

### Reproduction
One egg splits into four identical embryos, so it almost always has identical quadruplets; the embryo waits about 14 weeks before implanting; gestation about 4 months [1].

### Predators and threats
Pumas, jaguars, maned wolves, raptors and others; hunting and road traffic [1].

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
The armour shell, and identical quadruplets from a single egg [1].

### Ecological role and importance
An insect eater and a digger whose burrows other animals use (general knowledge, not from the sources above).

### Interesting facts
- Almost always born as identical quadruplets: one egg splits into four babies! [1]
- It can hold its breath for 6 minutes and swallows air to float across streams. [1]

### Uncertain or variable information
The ADW account describes the species before the 2024 split, including North American populations now called Dasypus mexicanus; the range and status on the card follow the 2025 IUCN assessment of the narrower species [1][2].
