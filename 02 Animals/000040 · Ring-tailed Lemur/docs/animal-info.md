## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Most terrestrial of all lemurs, easily recognized by its 60 cm tail banded with 13 black and 13 white rings, triangular black eye masks, pointed dark muzzle and grey-brown coat [1].

### Size and weight
Head-and-body length 39–46 cm, tail 56–63 cm, and weight 2.2–3.5 kg [1].

### Lifespan
16–19 years in the wild and up to 27 years in captivity [1].

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Opportunistic frugivore-folivore relying heavily on tamarind pods, figs, leaves, flowers, sap and occasional insects [1].

### Habitat
Gallery forests, dry deciduous woodlands, spiny bush and rocky outcrops in southern Madagascar [1, 2].

### Geographic distribution and native regions
Endemic to southern and southwestern Madagascar [2].

### Habits and behaviour
Diurnal and spends ~40% of its time on the ground; performs daily morning 'sun-worshipping' posture to warm up [1, 3].

### Social structure
Lives in multi-male, multi-female troops of 6–30 led by dominant females; males engage in 'stink fights' using wrist scent glands [1, 3].

### Reproduction
Gestation ~135 days; usually 1 infant (occasionally twins) born in September–October [1].

### Predators and threats
Severe habitat destruction from slash-and-burn agriculture, charcoal production, hunting and illegal pet trade [2].

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Scent glands on the wrists and chest allow chemical signaling during territorial disputes, while the long ringed tail serves as a visual beacon [1, 3].

### Ecological role and importance
Important seed disperser in Madagascar's arid southern forests [1, 2].

### Interesting facts
- Its iconic black-and-white tail is longer than its body and held high like a flag to keep the troop together. [1]
- In the morning sun, ring-tailed lemurs sit upright with arms spread wide like tiny sun-worshippers to warm up. [1,3]
