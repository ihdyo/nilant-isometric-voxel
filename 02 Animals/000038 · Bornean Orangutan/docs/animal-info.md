## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Second-largest great ape, distinguished by long coarse reddish-orange hair, disproportionately long arms (span up to 2.25 m), short bowed legs and broad cheek flanges in mature males [1].

### Size and weight
Standing height 1.1–1.4 m, arm span 2.0–2.25 m, and weight 30–50 kg in females and 50–100 kg in males [1].

### Lifespan
35–45 years in the wild and up to 59 years in human care [1].

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Over 60% fruit (durians, figs, mangosteens, jackfruit), plus young leaves, shoots, bark, honey, insects and bird eggs [1].

### Habitat
Lowland tropical dipterocarp forests and peat-swamp forests up to 1,500 m across Borneo [1, 2].

### Geographic distribution and native regions
Endemic to the island of Borneo (Kalimantan in Indonesia, Sabah and Sarawak in Malaysia, and Brunei) [2].

### Habits and behaviour
Diurnal and predominantly arboreal, moving via quadrumanous clambering and constructing leafy nests each evening [1, 3].

### Social structure
Semi-solitary; adult females travel with dependent offspring while flanged males use booming 'long calls' to space themselves [1].

### Reproduction
Gestation ~245 days; single infant nurses for up to 6–7 years—the longest interbirth interval of any land mammal [1].

### Predators and threats
Deforestation for oil palm and timber plantations, forest fires and illegal hunting [2].

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Opposable thumbs and big toes plus mobile hip and shoulder joints allow effortless suspension in the forest canopy, while populations pass down learned tool-use traditions [1, 3].

### Ecological role and importance
Keystone canopy gardener and long-distance seed disperser for Borneo's rainforest trees [1, 2].

### Interesting facts
- The largest tree-dwelling mammal on Earth, weaving a fresh leafy sleeping nest high in the canopy every night. [1,3]
- Its name comes from Malay 'orang hutan', meaning 'person of the forest'; arm span reaches over 2 meters. [1]
