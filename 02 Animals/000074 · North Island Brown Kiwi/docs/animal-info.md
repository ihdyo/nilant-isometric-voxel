## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Pear-shaped flightless nocturnal ratite with coarse hair-like streaked brown feathers, no external tail, vestigial wings, stout legs, and a long slender ivory bill. [1]

### Size and weight
Length 35–45 cm; weight 1.4–2.5 kg in males and 2.0–4.0 kg in females. [1]

### Lifespan
25–50 years in the wild. [1]

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Probes soil and leaf litter for earthworms, huhu grubs, crickets, centipedes, and fallen fruits. [1]

### Habitat
Damp broadleaf-podocarp forests, regenerating scrub, and managed pine forests. [1]

### Geographic distribution and native regions
Endemic to the North Island of New Zealand and several predator-free offshore islands. [1]

### Habits and behaviour
Strictly nocturnal; sleeps in burrows or hollow logs by day; surprisingly fast runner with strong kicking legs. [1]

### Social structure
Monogamous pairs maintaining territories of 5–40 hectares with loud shrill duets at night. [1]

### Reproduction
Female lays 1–2 huge eggs (400–450 g each); male incubates for ~75–85 days; chicks hatch fully feathered and fend for themselves. [1]

### Predators and threats
Vulnerable due to introduced mammalian predators (stoats, ferrets, dogs, and cats) preying on chicks and adults. [1, 2]

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Heavy marrow-filled bones (like a mammal), whiskers around the bill base, and tip-mounted nostrils. [1]

### Ecological role and importance
Soil invertebrate predator and seed disperser in New Zealand's unique flightless-bird ecosystems. [1]

### Interesting facts
- The only bird in the world with nostrils at the very tip of its long beak—allowing it to smell earthworms deep underground! [1]
- Females lay one of the largest eggs relative to body size of any bird, weighing up to 20% of the mother's body weight. [1]

### Uncertain or variable information
Five kiwi species are recognized in New Zealand; A. mantelli is the most numerous (~25,000 individuals).
