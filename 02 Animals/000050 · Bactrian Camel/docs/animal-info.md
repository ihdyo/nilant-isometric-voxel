## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Stocky, wool-covered camel with two prominent dorsal humps, a thick throat and foreleg mane, split prehensile upper lip, and broad two-toed padded feet. [1]

### Size and weight
Shoulder height 1.8–2.3 m (up to 2.5 m at the humps); head-and-body length 2.25–3.5 m; weight 300–1,000 kg. [1]

### Lifespan
35–50 years in the wild and captivity. [1]

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Browses tough, thorny, halophytic shrubs, dry grasses, and foliage; can drink over 100 litres of brackish or fresh water in minutes after deprivation. [1]

### Habitat
Arid gravel plains, sand dunes, and mountain steppes of the Gobi and Taklamakan deserts, up to 2,000 m elevation. [1]

### Geographic distribution and native regions
Wild populations survive only in small fragmented pockets in northwest China and southwest Mongolia (Gobi Desert). [1]

### Habits and behaviour
Diurnal and migratory, travelling in single-file caravans along seasonal water routes; tolerates extreme body-temperature fluctuations. [1]

### Social structure
Lives in small herds of 6–20 individuals led by a dominant adult male, or in solitary bachelor groups. [1]

### Reproduction
Gestation lasts 12–14 months (about 390–410 days), producing a single precocial calf weighing ~37 kg. [1]

### Predators and threats
Habitat loss from mining and agriculture, competition with domestic livestock, hybridization with domestic camels, and wolves preying on calves. [1, 2]

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Fat-filled humps allow localized insulation without trapping heat across the whole body; thick winter coat sheds in huge sheets each spring. [1]

### Ecological role and importance
Disperses hardy desert plant seeds and maintains open shrubland paths in hyper-arid ecosystems. [1]

### Interesting facts
- Its two humps store fat—not water—which converts into energy and hydration during long desert treks. [1]
- Double rows of long eyelashes and closable nostrils seal out fierce Gobi sandstorms. [1]

### Uncertain or variable information
Wild Bactrian camels are sometimes treated as a distinct species (Camelus ferus) by geneticists, though traditionally listed under Camelus bactrianus.
