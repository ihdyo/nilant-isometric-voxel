## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Forest-dwelling giraffid with a glossy dark chestnut-brown coat, horizontal white stripes on the hindquarters and upper legs, white lower stockings, pale cheeks, large ears and a 45 cm blue-black tongue [1].

### Size and weight
Head-and-body length 1.9–2.5 m, tail 30–42 cm, shoulder height 1.5–1.7 m, and weight 200–350 kg [1].

### Lifespan
20–30 years [1].

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Strict browser eating leaves, buds, shoots, ferns, fruits and fungi across more than 100 rainforest plant species [1, 3].

### Habitat
Dense, humid lowland tropical rainforests at 500–1,500 m elevation [1, 2].

### Geographic distribution and native regions
Endemic to the Democratic Republic of the Congo (notably the Ituri Forest and Virunga/Maiko regions) [2].

### Habits and behaviour
Diurnal, secretive and silent; navigates dense undergrowth along established paths using keen hearing and scent glands on the feet [1, 3].

### Social structure
Mostly solitary with overlapping home ranges [1, 3].

### Reproduction
Gestation 14–15 months (~440 days); single calf that hides in dense foliage for its first weeks [1].

### Predators and threats
Habitat loss from logging and agriculture, illegal bushmeat hunting and armed conflict [2].

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Oily, velvety coat repels torrential rainforest rain, and white leg stripes act as 'follow-me' signals for calves in dim understory light [1].

### Ecological role and importance
Specialized understory browser and seed disperser in the Congo Basin [1, 3].

### Interesting facts
- Despite its zebra-striped legs, the okapi is the giraffe's only living relative and shares its long blue tongue and skin-covered ossicones. [1]
- Its tongue is up to 45 cm long—long enough to wash its own eyelids and clean inside its ears. [1]
