## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
The largest penguin: black head, back and flippers, white belly, bright yellow-orange ear patches that fade into a pale yellow upper breast, and a long bill with an orange-pink stripe on the lower mandible [1].

### Size and weight
Height 101–132 cm; weight 25–45 kg, varying greatly through the breeding fast [1].

### Lifespan
15–20 years in the wild; 20–34 in captivity [1].

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Fish, krill and squid; adults eat 2–3 kg a day (up to 6 kg before the fast) [1].

### Habitat
Antarctic sea ice and the surrounding ocean; colonies breed on stable fast ice in winter temperatures of −40 to 0 °C with wind chill to −60 °C [1].

### Geographic distribution and native regions
Around the Antarctic continent; breeding colonies between 66° and 78° S [1].

### Habits and behaviour
Dives up to about 500 m and over 15 minutes in the ADW account [1]; the deepest recorded dive was 564 m (21.8 minutes in total) [3]. Birds huddle in groups of fewer than 200 up to 5,000–6,000 to save heat [1].

### Social structure
Colonial; huddles form in the breeding season [1].

### Reproduction
One egg; the male incubates it on his feet under a brood pouch for about 65 days while fasting, losing roughly 40% of his body mass [1].

### Predators and threats
Killer whales and leopard seals; south polar skuas take eggs and chicks [1].

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Huddling, dense plumage and fat, and the ability to fast for months [1].

### Ecological role and importance
A top predator of Antarctic fish and krill and prey of seals and orcas [1]; sea-ice loss from climate change is the main threat [2].

### Interesting facts
- Dads balance the egg on their feet for about 65 days of winter and eat nothing at all. [1]
- Huddles of up to 5,000 penguins share body heat through the polar night. [1]

### Uncertain or variable information
Status: Near Threatened (IUCN, BirdLife International 2020) [2]; the ADW page shows conflicting statuses [1]. Population projections under climate change carry wide uncertainty [1][2].
