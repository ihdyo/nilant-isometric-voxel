## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
A cat-sized climber with rusty red fur, dark red-black legs and belly, a white-marked face and ears, and a long, thick, ringed tail [1].

### Size and weight
Head and body 560–625 mm, tail 370–472 mm, weight 3.7–6.2 kg [1].

### Lifespan
Typically 8–10 years in the wild (maximum 14); about 13 years on average in captivity [1].

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Mainly bamboo leaves; also berries, blossoms, bird eggs and small leaves of other plants [1].

### Habitat
Temperate deciduous and coniferous forests with a bamboo understory and hollow trees, 2,200–4,800 m [1].

### Geographic distribution and native regions
The Himalaya: northern Myanmar, Nepal, Sikkim (India) and western Sichuan and Yunnan (China) [1].

### Habits and behaviour
Arboreal; it descends trees head first and moves nimbly between branches [1].

### Social structure
Solitary; adults rarely meet outside the mating season [1].

### Reproduction
Mating in early winter, most births in June; 1–4 young (about 2); gestation given as 134 days (116 days in AnAge) [1].

### Predators and threats
Snow leopards [1]; the main threats are habitat loss and fragmentation and poaching [2].

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
A flexible body and sharp claws let it climb down trees head first [1]; a 'false thumb' (an enlarged wrist bone) helps it grip bamboo (general knowledge, not covered by the sources above, to verify).

### Ecological role and importance
A bamboo-eating forest specialist; the national animal of Sikkim [1].

### Interesting facts
- Plot twist: it isn't a giant panda's cousin. Red pandas have a whole family all to themselves! [1]
- It climbs down trees head first, with a flexible spine and sharp claws. [1]

### Uncertain or variable information
Status: Endangered on the IUCN Red List (2015) [2]; the ADW page still gives Vulnerable [1]. Gestation figures differ between sources [1]. The species is sometimes split into two (Himalayan and Chinese red panda); this collection follows the IUCN treatment of one species [2].
