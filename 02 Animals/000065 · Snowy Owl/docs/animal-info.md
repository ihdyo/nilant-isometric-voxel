## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Large heavy-bodied white owl with round head (no ear tufts), golden-yellow eyes, black beak, and densely feathered legs and toes; adult males are nearly pure white while females and juveniles have dark barring. [1]

### Size and weight
Body length 52–71 cm; wingspan 1.25–1.50 m; weight 1.6–3.0 kg (heaviest North American owl). [1]

### Lifespan
10+ years in the wild (up to 28 in captivity). [1]

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Specialist predator of lemmings during breeding; takes voles, waterfowl, ptarmigan, and hares in winter. [1]

### Habitat
Circumpolar Arctic tundra in summer; open fields, airports, coastal marshes, and frozen lakes in winter. [1]

### Geographic distribution and native regions
Arctic regions of North America, Greenland, northern Europe, and Siberia. [1]

### Habits and behaviour
Diurnal hunter; perches on low hummocks to scan the tundra; undergoes irruptive southward migrations in winter. [1]

### Social structure
Solitary and territorial on breeding grounds. [1]

### Reproduction
Incubation ~32 days; clutch size tracks lemming abundance (3–11 eggs laid in a ground scrape). [1]

### Predators and threats
Arctic climate warming disrupting snow cover and lemming population cycles, plus collisions and rodenticides. [1, 2]

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Comb-like leading wing feathers muffle flight noise; dense plumage insulates down to −50 °C. [1]

### Ecological role and importance
Key predator driving and responding to Arctic lemming population cycles. [1]

### Interesting facts
- Unlike most owls, Snowy Owls hunt during the day—essential during 24-hour Arctic summer sunlight! [1]
- Thick feathers cover even their toes and claws like warm winter boots. [1]

### Uncertain or variable information
Global population estimates were revised downward in recent IUCN assessments, uplifting status to Vulnerable.
