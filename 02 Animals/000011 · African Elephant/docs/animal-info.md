## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Grey, creased skin up to 30 mm thick; huge triangular ears that help it cool down; a muscular trunk used for breathing, grasping and squirting water into the mouth; tusks in both sexes, up to 350 cm; calves are born without tusks [1].

### Size and weight
Shoulder height 2.2–2.6 m in females and 3.2–4 m in males; weight 2,000–3,500 kg in females and 4,500–6,100 kg in males: the largest land animal [1]. Total length is not given by ADW; about 6 m from trunk tip to tail for a big adult is general knowledge, used only for the voxel budget.

### Lifespan
60–70 years on average in the wild; up to 65 (perhaps 80) in captivity [1].

### Diet and feeding behaviour
A herbivore that feeds almost continuously on tree foliage, fruit, herbs, grasses, roots, twigs and bark, and needs roughly 50 US gallons (about 190 litres) of water a day [1].

### Habitat
Savannas, rainforests, woodlands, scrub forests, occasionally deserts and beaches, from sea level to 4,000 m; today mostly in protected areas [1].

### Geographic distribution and native regions
Patchy across central and southern Africa, with remnant populations as far as Guinea-Bissau and Ethiopia [1].

### Habits and behaviour
Walks about 10 km a day at about 6 km/h (runs up to 24 km/h) and sleeps about 4 hours in total; low rumbles around 20 Hz carry for more than 2 km [1].

### Social structure
Females live in matriarchal herds of 6 to 70 led by the largest, most dominant female; adult males live alone or in small bull groups [1].

### Reproduction
Gestation about 22 months (670 days); usually one calf of about 100 kg; females breed every 3–9 years [1].

### Predators and threats
Lions, wild dogs, hyenas and Nile crocodiles take young that fall behind; humans hunting for ivory are the main threat [1].

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Elephants call particular individuals with name-like calls: in a 2024 study, calls addressed to one elephant drew a stronger response from it than from others [3].

### Ecological role and importance
A keystone species and 'bioengineer': by breaking trees it opens habitat for reptiles and amphibians [1].

### Interesting facts
- Elephants seem to call each other by name: a 2024 study found rumbles meant for one elephant only. [3]
- Herds are led by the biggest, boss female, and up to 70 family members follow her. [1]

### Uncertain or variable information
Total body length (trunk to tail) is general knowledge, not from the sources, and is used only to size the voxel model. 'Name-like calls' is the study's interpretation of the data [3].
