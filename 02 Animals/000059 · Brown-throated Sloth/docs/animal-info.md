## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Arboreal mammal with shaggy grey-brown fur, pale face framed by dark eye stripes, three long curved claws (8–10 cm) on every limb, and a short stub tail. [1]

### Size and weight
Head-and-body length 42–80 cm; weight 2.3–6.3 kg. [1]

### Lifespan
20–30 years in the wild. [1]

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Folivore eating leaves, shoots, and flowers of Cecropia and other canopy trees; low-calorie diet requires a multi-week digestive cycle. [1]

### Habitat
Humid lowland and montane tropical forests from sea level to 2,400 m. [1]

### Geographic distribution and native regions
Honduras south through Nicaragua, Costa Rica, Panama, Colombia, Ecuador, Venezuela, Peru, Bolivia, and Brazil. [1]

### Habits and behaviour
Strictly arboreal; sleeps ~15–18 hours a day; descends to the forest floor only once a week to defecate and urinate. [1]

### Social structure
Solitary; overlapping home ranges high in the canopy. [1]

### Reproduction
Gestation ~6 months; single infant clings to its mother's belly for 5–6 months while learning which leaves are safe to eat. [1]

### Predators and threats
Deforestation, habitat fragmentation, power-line electrocution, and predation by harpy eagles and jaguars. [1, 2]

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Tendons lock claws closed effortlessly at rest; symbiotic algae provide camouflage and nutrients. [1]

### Ecological role and importance
Supports an entire micro-ecosystem of sloth moths, beetles, and fungi in its fur; fertilizes tree roots during weekly ground visits. [1]

### Interesting facts
- Algae grow in grooves along its fur, giving it green rainforest camouflage—and hosting a mini ecosystem of moths! [1]
- Extra neck vertebrae let it turn its head nearly 270 degrees while hanging upside down. [1]

### Uncertain or variable information
Historically misreported as sleeping 20 hours a day from captive observations; wild sloths sleep closer to 10–15 hours.
