## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Heaviest member of the weasel family, with a streamlined dark-brown body, pale silver-cream head, mitten-like front paws, webbed hind flippers and a flattened tail [1].

### Size and weight
Total length 1.0–1.5 m (tail 25–35 cm) and weight 14–45 kg [1].

### Lifespan
15–20 years in the wild [1].

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Consumes 25–30% of its body weight daily in sea urchins, abalones, clams, mussels, crabs and sea stars [1].

### Habitat
Shallow nearshore rocky reefs and kelp forests along the North Pacific rim [1, 2].

### Geographic distribution and native regions
Coastal waters of Russia (Commander and Kuril Islands), Alaska, British Columbia, Washington and California [2].

### Habits and behaviour
Spends almost its entire life at sea, floating on its back to eat, groom and sleep anchored in kelp [1].

### Social structure
Rests in same-sex social rafts of a dozen to over a hundred individuals [1].

### Reproduction
Gestation 6–8 months (including delayed implantation); usually one pup born at sea [1].

### Predators and threats
Oil spills, shark predation, fishing gear entanglement and disease [2].

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Lacks insulating blubber, relying instead on the world's densest fur coat and a metabolic rate 2–3 times higher than land mammals of similar size [1].

### Ecological role and importance
Classic keystone species: by preying on kelp-grazing sea urchins, sea otters preserve lush kelp forest ecosystems [3].

### Interesting facts
- It has the densest fur of any animal—up to 150,000 hairs per square centimeter—trapping air instead of blubber for warmth. [1]
- Sea otters wrap themselves in kelp fronds while sleeping at sea so they do not drift away. [1]
