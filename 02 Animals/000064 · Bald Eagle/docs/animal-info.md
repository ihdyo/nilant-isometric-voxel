## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Large sea eagle with dark brown body and wings, pure white head and tail, massive hooked yellow beak, unfeathered yellow legs, and powerful talons. [1]

### Size and weight
Body length 70–102 cm; wingspan 1.8–2.3 m; weight 3.0–6.3 kg (females ~25% larger than males). [1]

### Lifespan
20–30 years in the wild (over 40 in captivity). [1]

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Opportunistic carnivore specializing in fish snatched with talons; also takes waterbirds, small mammals, and carrion. [1]

### Habitat
Coastal shorelines, estuaries, major rivers, and lakes with tall perching and nesting trees. [1]

### Geographic distribution and native regions
North America from Alaska and Canada across the contiguous United States to northern Mexico. [1]

### Habits and behaviour
Soars effortlessly on thermal updrafts; pairs perform dramatic cartwheeling talon-locking courtship displays. [1]

### Social structure
Monogamous lifelong pairs reusing and expanding the same nest year after year; congregates at rich salmon runs in winter. [1]

### Reproduction
Incubation ~35 days; typically 1–3 eggs; eaglets fledge at 10–12 weeks. [1]

### Predators and threats
Recovered dramatically after DDT bans and Endangered Species Act protection; remaining threats include lead poisoning and collisions. [1, 2]

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Spicules (rough bumps) on foot pads and razor-curved talons grip slippery fish; eyesight is 4–5 times sharper than human vision. [1]

### Ecological role and importance
Apex aquatic-terrestrial predator and scavenger along North American waterways. [1]

### Interesting facts
- They build the largest tree nests of any bird—up to 4 metres deep and weighing over a metric tonne! [1]
- Juveniles have mottled brown heads and don't develop their iconic white head and tail until around age five. [1]

### Uncertain or variable information
Name 'bald' comes from the Old English word 'balde', meaning white-headed, not featherless.
