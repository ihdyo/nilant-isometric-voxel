## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Tall terrestrial raptor with crane-like legs, black thigh feathers ('breeches'), silver-grey plumage, bare orange-red face, long black-tipped nape crest, and elongated central tail feathers. [1]

### Size and weight
Standing height 1.2–1.5 m; wingspan 1.9–2.1 m; weight 3.3–4.3 kg. [1]

### Lifespan
10–15 years in the wild (up to 19 in captivity). [1]

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Carnivore specializing in snakes (including venomous vipers and cobras), large orthopterans, lizards, rodents, and ground birds. [1]

### Habitat
Open African savannas, grasslands, and semi-arid plains with scattered acacia trees for roosting. [1]

### Geographic distribution and native regions
Sub-Saharan Africa from Senegal and Somalia south to South Africa. [1]

### Habits and behaviour
Walks 20–30 km per day across open grass flushing prey; spreads wings as a shield while stomping snakes. [1]

### Social structure
Monogamous territorial pairs defending ranges of 20–50 km². [1]

### Reproduction
Builds a wide flat stick nest atop a thorny acacia; lays 1–3 eggs; incubation ~45 days. [1]

### Predators and threats
Endangered due to rapid conversion of African grasslands to cropland, overgrazing, frequent burning, and power-line collisions. [1, 2]

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Thick protective scales on lower legs resist snakebites; ultra-fast strike speed prevents snakes from retaliating. [1]

### Ecological role and importance
Top terrestrial snake and locust predator in African savanna ecosystems. [1]

### Interesting facts
- Instead of diving from the air, it hunts on foot—delivering kicks with 5 times its body weight in just 15 milliseconds! [1]
- Its name likely comes from its crest of long black quill feathers, resembling 19th-century clerks with quill pens tucked behind their ears. [1]

### Uncertain or variable information
Alternative etymology links the name to Arabic 'saqr-et-tair' (hunter-bird).
