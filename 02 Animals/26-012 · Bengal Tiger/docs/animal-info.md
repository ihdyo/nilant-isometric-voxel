## Identification

| | |
|---|---|
| Common name | Bengal Tiger (Indian tiger; the tiger subspecies of the Indian subcontinent) |
| Scientific name | *Panthera tigris tigris* |
| Taxonomy | Animalia › Chordata › Mammalia › Carnivora › Felidae › Panthera › *Panthera tigris* [1] |
| Conservation status | IUCN Red List: **Endangered** (EN, assessed 2021, published 2022) for the tiger as a species [2]; the Bengal tiger has no separate current assessment, and ADW lists Bengal tigers as endangered [1]. |

## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Reddish-orange coat with black vertical stripes that vary in size and spacing, a white spot on the back of each ear, white on the muzzle and belly [1].

### Size and weight
Males up to 3 m long and 225 kg, females up to 2.7 m and 135 kg [3]; across all tigers 1.98–3.7 m and 91–423 kg [1].

### Lifespan
Probably not more than 15 years in the wild, 16–18 in human care [3]; ADW gives 8–10 years in the wild for tigers in general [1].

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Medium to large prey such as pigs, deer, antelopes and buffalo; up to 40 kg of meat in one feeding [3]. Only 1 in 10 to 20 hunts succeeds [1].

### Habitat
Tropical jungle, brush, marshland and tall grassland [3].

### Geographic distribution and native regions
Fragmented areas of Bangladesh, Nepal, India, Bhutan and Myanmar (Burma) [3].

### Habits and behaviour
Solitary; males mark their territory with urine, droppings and scratch marks [3]. Tigers swim well and have been recorded crossing 29 km of water [1].

### Social structure
Solitary; the lasting bond is between a mother and her cubs [1].

### Reproduction
Gestation about 103 days; 1–7 cubs, born blind; cubs stay with the mother up to 3 years [1].

### Predators and threats
Poaching (tiger bones for traditional medicine), hunting and habitat loss [3][1].

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Stripes break up its outline in tall grass, so it can stalk close and pounce; it cannot chase prey far [3]. Bengal tigers have the longest canines of any living big cat, 7.5–10 cm [1].

### Ecological role and importance
A top predator of deer, pigs and wild cattle [1].

### Interesting facts
- Tigers are great swimmers: one was recorded crossing 29 km of water! [1]
- Bengal tigers have the longest fangs of any big cat alive: up to 10 cm, as long as your finger. [1]

### Uncertain or variable information
Wild lifespan differs between sources (8–10 years in ADW for tigers in general; 'probably not more than 15' for Bengal tigers at SeaWorld) [1][3]. The IUCN status shown is the species status [2].
