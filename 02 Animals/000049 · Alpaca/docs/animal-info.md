## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Smaller cousin of the llama descended from the wild vicuña, wrapped in dense, crimpy fleece with a woolly topknot, shorter straight spear-shaped ears and a rounded face [1, 2].

### Size and weight
Head-and-body length 1.2–1.5 m, tail 15–20 cm, shoulder height 81–99 cm, and weight 55–85 kg [1].

### Lifespan
15–20 years [1].

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Grazer specializing in moist Andean bofedal grasses and sedges [1, 3].

### Habitat
High-altitude Altiplano grasslands and wetlands at 3,500–5,000 m in the central Andes [1, 3].

### Geographic distribution and native regions
Peru, Bolivia, northern Chile and Ecuador, plus global fiber-farming herds [1, 2].

### Habits and behaviour
Gentle, diurnal and herd-oriented, humming softly to communicate with herd mates [1].

### Social structure
Lives in structured family herds with communal dung piles [1].

### Reproduction
Gestation 335–345 days; single cria [1].

### Predators and threats
Domesticated species not listed on the IUCN Red List [1, 2].

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Fine, hollow-core fiber provides exceptional thermal insulation against freezing Andean nights without heavy lanolin [1, 3].

### Ecological role and importance
Gentle grazer whose padded feet and lower incisors leave grass roots intact [1, 3].

### Interesting facts
- Bred for over 5,000 years from the wild vicuña specifically for its luxuriously soft, hypoallergenic fleece—not as a pack animal. [1,2]
- Unlike the llama's long banana-shaped ears, alpacas have shorter, straight spear-shaped ears and a fluffy wool topknot. [1]
