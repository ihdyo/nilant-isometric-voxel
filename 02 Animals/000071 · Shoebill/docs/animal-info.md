## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Prehistoric-looking tall wading bird with slate-grey plumage, wispy nape tuft, long black legs, forward-directed golden eyes, and a massive shoe-shaped hooked bill (24 cm long, 10 cm wide). [1]

### Size and weight
Standing height 1.1–1.4 m; wingspan 2.3–2.6 m; weight 4–7 kg. [1]

### Lifespan
35+ years in the wild (up to 50 in captivity). [1]

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Ambush piscivore specializing in marbled lungfish, catfish, tilapia, frogs, and water snakes. [1]

### Habitat
Dense, oxygen-poor freshwater swamps dominated by papyrus and reeds. [1]

### Geographic distribution and native regions
East-Central Africa: South Sudan (Sudd wetland), Uganda, Tanzania, Zambia, and Democratic Republic of the Congo. [1]

### Habits and behaviour
Patient 'statue' hunter; collapses forward ('collapsing' lunge) onto surfacing fish; soars on broad wings during the day. [1]

### Social structure
Solitary and territorial, defending 2–4 km² of swamp per pair. [1]

### Reproduction
Builds a 3 m wide floating vegetation nest; lays 1–3 eggs, though usually only one chick survives to fledge at ~105 days. [1]

### Predators and threats
Vulnerable (fewer than 5,000–8,000 adults left) due to wetland drainage, papyrus burning, oil exploration, and illegal live-bird trade. [1, 2]

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Sharp hooked nail at the tip of the upper mandible decapitates slippery lungfish and strips away vegetation. [1]

### Ecological role and importance
Apex wetland ambush predator in the Sudd and Bangweulu swamps. [1]

### Interesting facts
- It stands completely motionless like a prehistoric statue for hours, then lunges forward to snap up lungfish with its 24-cm clog bill. [1]
- Instead of singing, it greets its mate with rapid-fire bill-clattering that echoes across the swamp like a machine gun. [1]

### Uncertain or variable information
Despite its stork-like silhouette, DNA shows its closest living relatives are pelicans and hamerkops.
