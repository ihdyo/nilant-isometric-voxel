## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Giant rainforest hornbill with black body, yellow-tinted white neck, black-banded white tail, and a massive curved yellow bill topped by a concave double-pointed casque. [1]

### Size and weight
Length 95–130 cm; wingspan 1.5–1.8 m; weight 2.1–4.0 kg. [1]

### Lifespan
35–40 years in the wild (up to 50 in captivity). [1]

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Primarily frugivorous (wild figs make up over half the diet), supplemented with small vertebrates and arthropods during chick rearing. [1]

### Habitat
Old-growth evergreen and moist deciduous forests with emergent cavity trees up to 2,000 m. [1]

### Geographic distribution and native regions
Western Ghats and Himalayan foothills of India, Nepal, Bhutan, Myanmar, Thailand, Indochina, Peninsular Malaysia, and Sumatra (Indonesia). [1]

### Habits and behaviour
Beats heavy wings with a loud 'whooshing' sound audible hundreds of metres away; pairs duet loudly at dawn. [1]

### Social structure
Monogamous lifelong pairs; non-breeding birds form flocks of up to 40 at fruiting fig trees. [1]

### Reproduction
Female seals inside a high tree cavity for ~3–4 months; lays 1–2 eggs; male delivers hundreds of fruits daily through a narrow slit. [1]

### Predators and threats
Vulnerable due to logging of large old-growth nesting trees, forest fragmentation, and poaching. [1, 2]

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Fused first two neck vertebrae support the heavy hollow keratin casque, which acts as a vocal resonance chamber. [1]

### Ecological role and importance
Critical long-distance seed disperser ('farmers of the forest') for large-seeded rainforest trees. [1]

### Interesting facts
- During nesting, the female seals herself inside a tree hollow with mud and fruit pulp—leaving only a narrow slit for the male to pass food! [1]
- It literally 'paints' its white neck and bill bright yellow using golden preen oil from a gland near its tail. [1]

### Uncertain or variable information
Males have red irises ringed in black; females have pearly white irises ringed in red.
