## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Tiny iridescent hummingbird with metallic emerald-green upperparts, off-white belly, needle-slender black bill, forked dark tail, and—in adult males—a glowing ruby-red throat gorget. [1]

### Size and weight
Length 7–9 cm; wingspan 8–11 cm; weight 2–6 g (fattening up before migration). [1]

### Lifespan
3–5 years on average (record 9 years in the wild). [1]

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Nectarivore favouring red and orange tubular flowers (trumpet creeper, bee balm, cardinal flower), supplemented with small insects and spiders for protein. [1]

### Habitat
Deciduous and mixed forests, clearings, meadows, streamsides, and gardens. [1]

### Geographic distribution and native regions
Breeds across eastern North America from southern Canada to Florida; winters from southern Mexico to Costa Rica. [1]

### Habits and behaviour
Hyper-active diurnal hover-feeder; enters nightly torpor (dropping heart rate from 1,260 bpm to 50 bpm) to conserve energy. [1]

### Social structure
Solitary and pugnaciously territorial around rich flower patches and feeders. [1]

### Reproduction
Female builds a walnut-sized nest of thistle down and spider silk camouflaged with lichens; lays 2 pea-sized white eggs. [1]

### Predators and threats
Window collisions, outdoor cats, pesticide use, and climate shifts in flower bloom timing; overall increasing. [1, 2]

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Figure-eight wing stroke generates lift on both upstroke and downstroke; grooved forked tongue pumps nectar 13 times per second. [1]

### Ecological role and importance
Essential pollinator for dozens of native North American tubular wildflowers. [1]

### Interesting facts
- It beats its wings 53 times per second and is the only bird family able to hover motionless and fly backwards or upside down! [1]
- Despite weighing less than a nickel (3 grams), many migrate non-stop 800 km across the open Gulf of Mexico. [1]

### Uncertain or variable information
Females and juveniles lack the ruby gorget, showing a plain white throat instead.
