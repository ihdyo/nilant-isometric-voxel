## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Stocky heritage cattle breed famous for its long wavy ginger-red coat, thick forehead fringe covering the eyes, and wide symmetrical upward-curving horns. [1]

### Size and weight
Shoulder height 1.06–1.30 m; body length 2.0–2.5 m; weight 420–500 kg in cows and 650–800 kg in bulls. [1]

### Lifespan
15–20 years (longer than most commercial cattle breeds). [1]

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Hardy browser and grazer thriving on rough mountain grasses, heather, rushes, and browse. [1]

### Habitat
Wet, windswept hills, moorlands, and conservation pastures across Scotland and temperate regions worldwide. [1]

### Geographic distribution and native regions
Originated in the Scottish Highlands and Outer Hebrides; now raised globally for hardy grazing and conservation. [1]

### Habits and behaviour
Docile, calm, and maternal; calves are sheltered in heather while mothers graze. [1]

### Social structure
Forms small family herds (historically called 'folds' in Scotland). [1]

### Reproduction
Gestation ~283 days; typically one fluffy calf per year. [1]

### Predators and threats
Domesticated breed with secure global population; not evaluated on the IUCN Red List. [1, 2]

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Dual-layer coat sheds heavy rain and insulates against sub-zero gales; uses horns to clear snow from winter forage. [1]

### Ecological role and importance
Widely used in conservation grazing to restore heathlands and wetlands by eating woody scrub. [1]

### Interesting facts
- Their iconic shaggy fringe—called a 'dossan' in Scots—shields their eyes from driving rain, snow and midges. [1]
- They grow a double coat (oily outer hair over a downy undercoat) instead of thick back fat to stay warm. [1]

### Uncertain or variable information
As a domesticated breed (Bos taurus), it has no wild IUCN Red List category.
