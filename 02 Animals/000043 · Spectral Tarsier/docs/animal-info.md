## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Tiny nocturnal primate with velvety grey-buff fur, enormous forward-facing amber eyes, mobile bat-like ears, long disk-tipped fingers and a long tail tufted at the tip [1].

### Size and weight
Head-and-body length 9.5–14 cm, tail 20–26 cm, and weight 100–150 g [1].

### Lifespan
10–12 years [1].

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Strictly faunivorous/insectivorous—hunting live insects, spiders, small lizards, frogs and bats by leaping onto prey [1].

### Habitat
Lowland tropical forests, secondary growth, bamboo groves and mangroves on Sulawesi and adjacent islands [1, 2].

### Geographic distribution and native regions
Endemic to Sulawesi and nearby islands (Selayar, Peleng, Togian) in Indonesia [2].

### Habits and behaviour
Nocturnal vertical clinger-and-leaper; family groups sing synchronized dawn duets upon returning to sleeping trees [1].

### Social structure
Lives in small monogamous or polygynous family groups of 2–6 occupying strangler fig sleeping sites [1].

### Reproduction
Gestation ~178 days; single large, furred infant born with open eyes and able to climb within days [1].

### Predators and threats
Deforestation, agricultural expansion, bushfires and illegal pet capture [2].

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Enormous eyes maximize light capture at night, and a specialized cervical spine permits ~180° head rotation [1, 3].

### Ecological role and importance
Major nocturnal arthropod predator in Sulawesi lowland forests [1].

### Interesting facts
- Each eyeball is as large as its entire brain; because its eyes cannot move in their sockets, it rotates its head nearly 180° like an owl. [1,3]
- Elongated ankle bones (tarsals) give tarsiers their name and launch them across 3-meter leaps between tree trunks. [1]
