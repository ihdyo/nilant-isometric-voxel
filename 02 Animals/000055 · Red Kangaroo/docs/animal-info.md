## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Largest marsupial; rusty red-brown males and blue-grey females, long pointed ears, small forelimbs, huge hind feet, and a thick muscular tail. [1]

### Size and weight
Standing height up to 1.8 m; head-and-body 1.3–1.6 m plus 1.0–1.2 m tail; weight 22–92 kg. [1]

### Lifespan
12–18 years in the wild (over 20 in captivity). [1]

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Grazes green grasses and forbs; concentrates urine and extracts moisture from vegetation to survive drought. [1]

### Habitat
Open arid and semi-arid plains, mulga scrub, and grassland across central Australia. [1]

### Geographic distribution and native regions
Endemic to mainland Australia, widespread across the arid interior. [1]

### Habits and behaviour
Crepuscular and nocturnal; rests in shade during midday heat and licks forearms to cool by evaporation. [1]

### Social structure
Lives in loose groups ('mobs') of 2–10 led by a dominant male, coalescing into larger herds near green feed. [1]

### Reproduction
Gestation ~33 days; tiny bean-sized joey crawls into the pouch and nurses for ~8 months; exhibits embryonic diapause. [1]

### Predators and threats
Dingo predation on young, drought cycles, and road collisions; overall abundant. [1, 2]

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Elastic Achilles tendons store and return 70% of jump energy, making high-speed hopping remarkably efficient. [1]

### Ecological role and importance
Primary native herbivore of the Australian outback, shaping grass composition. [1]

### Interesting facts
- The world's largest living marsupial—a single bound can cover 9 metres at speeds over 60 km/h. [1]
- Its thick muscular tail acts like a 'fifth leg' when walking slowly and a counterbalance mid-leap. [1]

### Uncertain or variable information
Previously classified in the genus Macropus (Macropus rufus) before revision to Osphranter.
