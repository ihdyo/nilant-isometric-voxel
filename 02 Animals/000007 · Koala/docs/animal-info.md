## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
A stocky tree-dweller with thick grey fur, a white chest, large round fluffy ears and a big dark leathery nose; cheek pouches store unchewed food [1].

### Size and weight
Southern animals average 78 cm (males) and 72 cm (females) in length; weight 11.8 / 7.9 kg in the south and 6.5 / 5.1 kg in the north (males / females) [1].

### Lifespan
May live past 10 years in the wild; over 20 years reported in captivity [1].

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Eucalyptus leaves only: about 20 of some 350 eucalyptus species are eaten and 5 preferred; an adult eats about 500 g a day [1].

### Habitat
Eucalyptus forest below about 600 m [1].

### Geographic distribution and native regions
Eastern Australia from northern Queensland to south-western Victoria; introduced to some islands [1].

### Habits and behaviour
Rests or sleeps up to 18 hours a day; cools itself by licking its forearms, as it has no sweat glands [1].

### Social structure
Mostly solitary and sedentary in overlapping home ranges, one animal per tree [1].

### Reproduction
Gestation 25–35 days; the joey spends 5–7 months in the pouch, then rides on the mother's back until about 11 months [1].

### Predators and threats
Relatively defenceless on the ground; trees keep it out of reach of predators [1]. Threats are habitat clearing, disease (chlamydia), fire and drought [1][2].

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
A caecum up to four times its body length ferments the fibrous, low-protein leaves [1].

### Ecological role and importance
A specialist browser of eucalyptus canopies [1].

### Interesting facts
- It naps up to 18 hours a day. A leafy, low-energy diet means lots of rest! [1]
- No sweat glands, no problem: it cools off by licking its arms. [1]

### Uncertain or variable information
IUCN lists Vulnerable (2016) [2]; Australian listings differ by state and changed after 2020 (Endangered in Queensland, New South Wales and the ACT under national law since 2022: general knowledge, to verify against the Australian Government listing). The ADW page still gives Least Concern [1].
