## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
The tallest mammal: very long legs and neck, a short sloping body, a coat of brown patches separated by cream lines, and two skin-covered bony horns (ossicones) on the head [1].

### Size and weight
Males up to 5.7 m to the horns and 3.3 m at the shoulder, neck about 2.4 m; bulls up to 1,930 kg, cows up to 1,180 kg; females 0.7–1 m shorter [1].

### Lifespan
About 10–15 years in the wild; 20–25 in captivity [1].

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Mainly acacia leaves; an adult male may eat up to 66 kg a day (as little as 7 kg in poor areas); the tongue is about 45 cm long [1].

### Habitat
Savannas, grasslands, open woodland and scrub [1].

### Geographic distribution and native regions
Africa south of the Sahara; reintroduced to reserves in South Africa [1].

### Habits and behaviour
Runs at 32–60 km/h; rests and sleeps mostly standing [1].

### Social structure
Loose, changing herds of 10–20 (up to 70) [1].

### Reproduction
Gestation about 457 days; the calf is about 2 m tall at the shoulder and drops about 2 m to the ground at birth [1].

### Predators and threats
Mainly lions; also leopards, hyenas and crocodiles at waterholes [1].

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Seven elongated neck vertebrae, ossicones, a long prehensile tongue for browsing [1].

### Ecological role and importance
A browser of the tree canopy that shapes acacia woodland [1].

### Interesting facts
- That huge neck has just 7 bones, the same number as yours! [1]
- Calves drop about 2 m to the ground at birth. Welcome to the world! [1]

### Uncertain or variable information
IUCN treats one species, Vulnerable since 2016 [2]; the ADW page still gives Least Concern [1]. Some researchers split giraffes into four species (general knowledge, not from the sources above); this collection follows the IUCN treatment.
