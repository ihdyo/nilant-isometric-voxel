## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Purple-grey to slate skin, brownish pink around the eyes and ears; eyes, ears and nostrils sit high on the head so it can stay almost submerged; canine tusks up to 50 cm; jaws open up to 150 degrees [1].

### Size and weight
2.9–5.05 m long including a tail of about 35 cm, 1.5–1.65 m tall, 1,300–3,200 kg (up to 4,500 kg recorded) [1].

### Lifespan
Around 55 years in the wild and in captivity; the oldest exceeded 61 years [1].

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Short grasses, shoots and reeds: about 40 kg a night (1–1.5 % of its weight), grazed in 4–5 hours over 3–4 km [1].

### Habitat
Shallow lakes, rivers and swamps; water about 2 m deep is preferred [1].

### Geographic distribution and native regions
Sub-Saharan Africa [1].

### Habits and behaviour
Rests in the water most of the day and comes out at dusk to graze; can reach 30 km/h on land [1].

### Social structure
Pods of about 20 to 100; females lead the resting pools, males line the banks [1].

### Reproduction
One calf of 22–55 kg after about 324 days (ADW also lists 234; see below), born in or near water; calves nurse underwater [1].

### Predators and threats
Hunting for meat and ivory, and habitat loss; lions, hyenas and crocodiles sometimes take young [1].

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
It secretes a red-orange 'blood sweat' that works as a sunscreen and resists bacteria; its pigments were identified in 2004 [1][3].

### Ecological role and importance
A keystone species: its paths and wallows make habitat for smaller organisms [1].

### Interesting facts
- Hippos make their own sunscreen: a red skin oil that blocks the sun and fights germs. [1, 3]
- Calves are born in or near water and even nurse underwater, ears folded and nostrils shut. [1]

### Uncertain or variable information
ADW gives two gestation figures (324 days in the text, 234 in the data box) and two weight ranges [1]; the card uses the text's figures.
