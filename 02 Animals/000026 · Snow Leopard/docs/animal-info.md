## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Dense smoky-grey fur patterned with dark rosettes and spots; a small rounded head with short ears; broad, fur-covered paws that act as snowshoes; and an exceptionally thick tail 80–100 cm long [1].

### Size and weight
Head-and-body length 100–130 cm, tail 80–100 cm, shoulder height about 60 cm, and weight 22–55 kg [1].

### Lifespan
15–18 years in the wild and up to 21 years in human care [1].

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Carnivore preying primarily on blue sheep (bharal), Siberian ibex, argali, marmots, pikas, hares and upland birds [1].

### Habitat
Alpine meadows, rocky ridges and steep cliffs between 3,000 and 5,500 m elevation across twelve Central and South Asian countries [1, 2].

### Geographic distribution and native regions
High mountain ranges of Central and South Asia including the Himalayas, Karakoram, Pamir, Tian Shan and Altai [2].

### Habits and behaviour
Mostly crepuscular and solitary, capable of leaps up to 15 m across rocky ravines; uses its long tail for balance and warmth [1].

### Social structure
Solitary outside the brief winter mating season and mother-cub rearing period [1].

### Reproduction
Gestation lasts 90–105 days, yielding 1–5 (typically 2–3) cubs born in rocky dens in spring [1].

### Predators and threats
Poaching for pelts and bones, retaliatory killing over livestock depredation, habitat fragmentation and climate change [2].

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Enlarged nasal cavities warm frigid mountain air before it reaches the lungs, while wide fur-padded paws distribute weight over snow [1].

### Ecological role and importance
Apex predator regulating mountain ungulate populations and maintaining alpine pasture balance [1, 2].

### Interesting facts
- Its thick tail is nearly as long as its body and works as a balance pole on cliffs and a scarf while sleeping. [1]
- Unlike lions and tigers, snow leopards cannot roar because their vocal cords lack a thick elastic pad. [3]
