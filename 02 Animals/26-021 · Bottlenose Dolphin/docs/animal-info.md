## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
A streamlined body with flippers, a tall curved dorsal fin and tail flukes; dark grey to black on the sides with a white belly that can look slightly pink [1].

### Size and weight
Males 2.44–3.81 m, females 2.28–3.66 m; males about 500 kg, females about 250 kg [1].

### Lifespan
Males typically 40–45 years, females over 50 [1].

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Fish and invertebrates near shore, squid and open-sea fish offshore; prey 5–30 cm long; 4.5–16 kg a day [1].

### Habitat
Bays, estuaries, sounds, open shorelines and large estuarine rivers; mostly warm waters [1].

### Geographic distribution and native regions
Every ocean except the polar seas, and the Mediterranean [1].

### Habits and behaviour
Swims up to 30 km/h (3–6 km/h on average); holds its breath up to 4.5 minutes; finds prey by echolocation; sleeps one brain half at a time [1].

### Social structure
Groups from a few to over 100 that split and join again [1].

### Reproduction
Gestation about 12 months; one calf, nursed 18–20 months [1].

### Predators and threats
Bull, tiger and dusky sharks take calves and small adults; fishing gear and pollution [1].

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Echolocation and a signature whistle each dolphin keeps for life [1].

### Ecological role and importance
A top predator of fish and squid near the coast [1].

### Interesting facts
- Every dolphin has its own signature whistle, a bit like a name, and keeps it for life. [1]
- It sleeps with half its brain at a time, so it never forgets to breathe. [1]

### Uncertain or variable information
Reported wild lifespans vary (averages of 25 and 35 years) [1].
