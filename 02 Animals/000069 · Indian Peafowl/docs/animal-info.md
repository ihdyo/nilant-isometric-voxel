## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Large ground-dwelling pheasant; males display an iridescent royal-blue neck, fan-tipped head crest, and a 1.5 m golden-green train studded with bronze-and-blue eyespots. [1]

### Size and weight
Males 1.8–2.3 m total length (including train), 4–6 kg; females 0.9–1.0 m, 2.7–4 kg. [1]

### Lifespan
15–20 years in the wild (up to 25+ in captivity). [1]

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Omnivorous ground forager eating seeds, shoots, flowers, insects, worms, frogs, and small snakes. [1]

### Habitat
Dry and moist deciduous forests, scrub jungle, cultivated fields, and temple groves up to 1,800 m. [1]

### Geographic distribution and native regions
Native to India, Sri Lanka, and Pakistan; introduced widely around the world. [1]

### Habits and behaviour
Forages on the ground by day and roosts high in tall trees at night; capable of strong bursts of flight despite the heavy train. [1]

### Social structure
Polygynous; males defend display arenas ('leks') visited by harems of 2–5 peahens. [1]

### Reproduction
Incubation ~28 days; 4–8 buff eggs laid in a ground scrape; chicks follow the mother immediately. [1]

### Predators and threats
Locally affected by agricultural pesticides and habitat loss, but protected culturally and legally across India. [1, 2]

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Photonic crystal nanostructures in feather barbules produce vivid iridescent blues, greens, and golds. [1]

### Ecological role and importance
Seed disperser and predator of insects and small snakes in forest-edge habitats. [1]

### Interesting facts
- Its famous 1.5-metre 'tail' is actually made of over 200 elongated upper-tail covert feathers—each tipped with a shimmering eyespot! [1]
- When displaying, the male rattles his feathers at 26 Hz, creating low-frequency infrasound vibrations felt by nearby peahens. [1]

### Uncertain or variable information
The related Green Peafowl (Pavo muticus) native to Java and Southeast Asia is Endangered, unlike the Least Concern Indian Peafowl.
