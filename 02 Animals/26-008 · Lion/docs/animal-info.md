## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
A large, muscular cat with a tawny coat, a tufted tail tip and, in adult males, a mane around the head and neck; males are the only cats with manes [1].

### Size and weight
Total length 2.4–3.3 m including a 0.6–1 m tail; males average 189 kg (record 272 kg), females 126 kg; standing height about 1.2 m (males) [1].

### Lifespan
Around 14 years in the wild (females in the Serengeti up to 18); some reach 30 in captivity [1].

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Mainly large hoofed mammals such as Thomson's gazelles, zebras, impalas and wildebeest; some prides take buffalo and giraffe; also smaller animals and carrion. Females do most of the hunting [1].

### Habitat
Plains and savanna with plenty of prey; also open woodland, scrub, mountains and semi-desert, but not rainforest or true desert [1].

### Geographic distribution and native regions
Sub-Saharan Africa outside deserts and rainforests; the Asiatic lion survives only in and around the Gir Forest, India [1].

### Habits and behaviour
Rests about 20 hours a day where prey is plentiful [1].

### Social structure
Lives in prides of 2–40 (about 13 in Kruger and the Serengeti) [1].

### Reproduction
Breeds year-round; gestation about 109 days; 1–6 cubs (about 3); weaned at 7–10 months [1]. Males that take over a pride often kill unweaned cubs [1].

### Predators and threats
No natural predators as adults; humans (conflict, prey loss, habitat loss) are the main threat [1][2].

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Cooperative hunting by the pride's females; the mane signals a male's sex and condition [1].

### Ecological role and importance
An apex predator of African savannas [1].

### Interesting facts
- Every lion has its own pattern of whisker spots, like a fingerprint. [1]
- Lions are the only cats with manes. [1]

### Uncertain or variable information
The IUCN global status is Vulnerable [2]; regional subpopulations differ (the West African subpopulation is Critically Endangered) [2]. Wild lifespan figures vary between sources [1].
