## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Close relative of the Indian rhinoceros, featuring dusky grey skin with deep folds creating an armor-plated look, a pointed prehensile upper lip and a single short horn in males [1].

### Size and weight
Head-and-body length 3.1–3.2 m, tail 60–70 cm, shoulder height 1.4–1.7 m, and weight 900–2,300 kg [1].

### Lifespan
30–40 years [1].

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Browser eating shoots, twigs, young leaves and fallen fruit across more than 100 plant species [1].

### Habitat
Lowland tropical rainforests, river floodplains and coastal mud wallows [1, 2].

### Geographic distribution and native regions
Confined exclusively to Ujung Kulon National Park on the western tip of Java, Indonesia (~76 individuals) [2, 3].

### Habits and behaviour
Solitary, shy and mostly active at night and dawn, spending hours submerged in mud wallows [1, 3].

### Social structure
Solitary except for mothers with calves and brief mating encounters [1].

### Reproduction
Gestation ~16 months; single calf born every 4–5 years [1].

### Predators and threats
Extreme vulnerability of a single tiny population to disease, invasive Arenga palm, tsunamis/volcanic eruptions and poaching [2, 3].

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Pointed prehensile upper lip grasps branches and pulls saplings down to browse [1].

### Ecological role and importance
Creates forest clearings and wallows that maintain microhabitat diversity in Ujung Kulon [1, 3].

### Interesting facts
- One of the rarest large mammals on Earth, with the entire wild population surviving in Ujung Kulon National Park in Java, Indonesia. [2]
- Deep skin folds give it a riveted armor-plated appearance, and only males grow a single horn (usually under 25 cm). [1]

### Uncertain or variable information
Total nose-to-tail length of 3.5 m combines body and tail lengths [1].
