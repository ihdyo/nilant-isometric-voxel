## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Wild axolotls are dark, mottled brownish-green; captive colour forms range from wild type to albino. Adults keep feathery external gills and a finned tail [1]. The model shows the pale pink (leucistic) form common in aquariums.

### Size and weight
About 20–23 cm long on average, some over 30 cm; 60–110 g [1].

### Lifespan
5–6 years in the laboratory, some 10–15 years [1].

### Diet and feeding behaviour
A top predator in its lake: molluscs, fish, arthropods, insects, amphibians, worms, zooplankton and even other axolotls [1].

### Habitat
Large, high-altitude lakes; today only the canals and swamps left of Lake Xochimilco, at about 2,274 m. Lake Chalco was drained for drinking water [1].

### Geographic distribution and native regions
The Valley of Mexico, near Mexico City [1].

### Habits and behaviour
Paedomorphic: it keeps its larval gills and tail fin as an adult and rarely, if ever, changes into a land form in the wild; solitary; finds prey by electric fields, sight and smell; known for remarkable regeneration [1].

### Social structure
Solitary [1].

### Reproduction
Breeds from about March to June; females lay 100–300 eggs singly on rocks or plants; eggs hatch in 10–14 days; no parental care [1].

### Predators and threats
Large introduced fish, cannibalism and the loss of almost all its habitat [1].

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
It keeps its gills for life and regrows lost body parts [1].

### Ecological role and importance
A top predator of its lake [1].

### Interesting facts
- It keeps its feathery baby gills all its life and is famous for regrowing lost body parts. [1]
- Almost every lab axolotl descends from just 33 shipped from Mexico to Paris in 1864! [1]

### Uncertain or variable information
The colour shown is the aquarium form, not the wild colour [1]. Lifespan figures come from captive animals [1].
