## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Small, slender mongoose with a sandy-tan coat, dark dorsal bands, dark eye rings, small black crescent ears, a pointed snout and a thin black-tipped tail [1].

### Size and weight
Head-and-body length 25–35 cm, tail 17.5–25 cm, and weight 620–970 g [1].

### Lifespan
12–14 years in captivity; typically 5–8 years in the wild [1].

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Insectivore-carnivore eating beetles, caterpillars, scorpions, spiders, centipedes, lizards, small snakes, eggs and roots [1].

### Habitat
Arid open plains, savannas and scrublands with firm soils suitable for digging complex burrows [1, 2].

### Geographic distribution and native regions
South Africa, Namibia, Botswana and southern Angola [2].

### Habits and behaviour
Strictly diurnal; takes turns standing upright on hind legs and tail as a sentry while clan members dig for prey [1, 3].

### Social structure
Highly social cooperative breeders living in clans (mobs) of 10–30 individuals led by a dominant pair [1].

### Reproduction
Gestation about 70 days; 2–5 pups raised cooperatively by the entire clan [1].

### Predators and threats
Widespread and common across southern African arid zones [2].

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Immunity to many scorpion venoms and dark eye rings that reduce desert sun glare; closes ears while digging [1].

### Ecological role and importance
Major arthropod predator and soil aerator in Kalahari ecosystems [1].

### Interesting facts
- While the mob digs for insects, one sentry stands upright on its tail and calls distinct alarms for eagles versus jackals. [3]
- Dark rings around its eyes act like built-in sunglasses against the bright Kalahari sun. [1]
