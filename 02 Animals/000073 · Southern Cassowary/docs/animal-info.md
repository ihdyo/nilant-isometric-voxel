## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Flightless ratite with coarse hair-like black plumage, tall brown-grey keratin casque, vivid cobalt-blue bare neck, twin red wattles, and heavy three-toed legs. [1]

### Size and weight
Standing height 1.5–1.8 m; weight 29–58 kg (females larger and more brightly coloured than males). [1]

### Lifespan
40–50 years in the wild (up to 60 in captivity). [1]

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Frugivore swallowing whole fruits up to the size of a mango; seeds pass intact through its gentle digestive tract. [1]

### Habitat
Lowland and foothill tropical rainforests, gallery forests, and palm swamps up to 1,100 m. [1]

### Geographic distribution and native regions
Southern New Guinea (Papua/Indonesia and Papua New Guinea), Seram, Aru Islands, and Cape York / Wet Tropics of Queensland, Australia. [1]

### Habits and behaviour
Shy and solitary; produces the lowest-frequency bird calls known (~23 Hz infrasound) that boom through dense jungle. [1]

### Social structure
Solitary; males alone incubate the eggs and raise the striped chicks for 9 months. [1]

### Reproduction
Female lays 3–5 large green eggs in a forest-floor leaf nest and departs; male incubates for ~50 days and guards chicks. [1]

### Predators and threats
Habitat clearing, road collisions in Queensland, and hunting in New Guinea. [1, 2]

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Spongy keratin casque protects the skull when crashing through dense undergrowth at 50 km/h and may amplify low booms. [1]

### Ecological role and importance
Irreplaceable keystone seed disperser for large-fruited rainforest trees across Sahul. [1]

### Interesting facts
- The world's third-tallest and second-heaviest living bird—each three-toed foot sports a 12-centimetre dagger-like inner claw! [1]
- As a 'rainforest gardener', over 200 tropical tree species depend on cassowaries to swallow and disperse their large seeds. [1]

### Uncertain or variable information
Reclassified from Vulnerable to Least Concern globally due to large intact forests in New Guinea, though Australian populations remain Endangered.
