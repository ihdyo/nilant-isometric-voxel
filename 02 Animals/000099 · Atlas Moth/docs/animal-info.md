## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Immense reddish-brown wings patterned with cream bands, triangular translucent 'window' spots, hooked snake-head forewing tips, a furry thorax, and wide feathery antennae. [1].

### Size and weight
Wingspan 24–27 cm (among the largest moths in the world by wing area); wingspan 24–27 cm, weight 4–8 g [1].

### Lifespan
1–2 weeks [1].

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Citrus, cinnamon, and rambutan leaves as caterpillars; adults have no functional mouthparts and do not eat. [1].

### Habitat
Tropical and subtropical forests across Southeast Asia, southern China, India, and the Malay Archipelago. [1].

### Habits and behaviour
Both forewing tips are shaped and patterned like snake heads to startle hungry birds and lizards. [1].

### Unique adaptations
Adult moths live entirely on fat reserves stored during their caterpillar stage, surviving only one to two weeks. [1].
