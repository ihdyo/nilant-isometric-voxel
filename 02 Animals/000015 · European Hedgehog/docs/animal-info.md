## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
About 5,000 hollow, grooved spines 2–2.5 cm long, banded brown and black with white tips and bases; coarse yellow-brown hair on the face, throat, belly and legs; short legs, five padded, clawed toes [1].

### Size and weight
Head and body 135–265 mm (ADW also gives 135–165), tail about 20 mm, 800–1,200 g [1].

### Lifespan
Up to 6 years in the wild, up to 10 in captivity [1].

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Mostly insects (beetles, ants, bees, wasps, earwigs, moths and butterflies), plus snails, eggs, small vertebrates, carrion, seeds, nuts and fruit [1].

### Habitat
Field edges, hedgerows, gardens, cemeteries, parks and farmland, from sea level to 2,400 m [1].

### Geographic distribution and native regions
Europe into central Asia, the Azores and Scandinavia; introduced to New Zealand [1].

### Habits and behaviour
Nocturnal and solitary; rolls into a ball with all spines up when threatened; hibernates from about October to April, waking every 1–2 weeks for 1–2 days to forage and urinate [1].

### Social structure
Solitary and non-territorial; meets others mainly to mate [1].

### Reproduction
Gestation about 35 days; 4–6 young, often two litters a year; the spines lie under fluid-filled skin at birth and appear about 24 hours later [1].

### Predators and threats
Dogs, foxes, badgers, large owls; local populations are declining fast for reasons not fully understood [1][2].

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
The spiny coat and the panniculus carnosus muscle that pulls it into a ball [1].

### Ecological role and importance
An insect and snail eater of gardens and farmland [1].

### Interesting facts
- In winter it hibernates, but wakes every week or two for a day of snacks and a wee. [1]
- Hoglets are born with their spines tucked under soft skin; they pop up within a day. [1]

### Uncertain or variable information
ADW gives two head-and-body ranges (135–265 mm and 135–165 mm) [1]; 'hoglets' (the young) is everyday usage, not from the source.
