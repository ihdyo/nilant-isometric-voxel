## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Scarlet head and shoulders, yellow back and mid-wing feathers, blue wing tips and tail feathers; short white feathers around light yellow eyes; the bill light on top and black below; black legs [1].

### Size and weight
About 89 cm long, the tail a third to a half of it; about 1–1.2 kg [1].

### Lifespan
40–50 years typical in the wild; captive birds average 64 and large macaws may reach 75 [1].

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Mainly fruit and nuts, sometimes nectar and flowers; it eats unripe fruit and some clay from riverbanks, which helps it digest the tannins [1].

### Habitat
Rainforest canopy below 1,000 m [1].

### Geographic distribution and native regions
Southern Mexico, Central America and South America to north-eastern Argentina, most common in the Amazon basin [1].

### Habits and behaviour
Sleeps in flocks; pairs mate for life, lick each other's faces and preen [1].

### Social structure
Monogamous pairs inside larger flocks [1].

### Reproduction
2–4 white eggs, incubated 24–25 days mostly by the female; young may stay with the parents 1–2 years [1].

### Predators and threats
Forest loss, poaching and the illegal pet trade [1].

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
A beak that cracks very tough nuts; a preference for the left foot when handling food [1].

### Ecological role and importance
A fruit and seed eater of the canopy [1].

### Interesting facts
- Most scarlet macaws are lefties: they hold their food with the left foot. [1]
- Pairs stay together for life and preen each other's feathers. [1]

### Uncertain or variable information
ADW's wild lifespan figures disagree (up to 33 years in one table, 40–50 typical in another) [1].
