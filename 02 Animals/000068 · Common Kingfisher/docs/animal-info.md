## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Sparrow-sized jewel-like kingfisher with brilliant iridescent turquoise-blue upperparts, vivid rufous-orange underparts, white throat/neck flashes, tiny red feet, and a long dagger bill. [1]

### Size and weight
Length 16–17 cm; wingspan 24–26 cm; weight 34–46 g. [1]

### Lifespan
6–15 years (high first-year mortality in harsh winters). [1]

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Piscivore eating small fish up to 12 cm long plus aquatic beetle and dragonfly larvae; stuns fish against a branch before swallowing headfirst. [1]

### Habitat
Slow-moving freshwater streams, rivers, ponds, and estuaries with overhanging perches and sandy banks. [1]

### Geographic distribution and native regions
Widespread across Europe, North Africa, and Asia east to Japan and Indonesia. [1]

### Habits and behaviour
Watches motionless from a low branch, then plunges vertically up to 1 m underwater with eyes shielded by nictitating membranes. [1]

### Social structure
Solitary and fiercely territorial outside the spring/summer breeding season. [1]

### Reproduction
Excavates a 60–90 cm tunnel in a vertical riverbank; lays 6–7 glossy white eggs; raises 2–3 broods per year. [1]

### Predators and threats
Severe winter freezes that ice over rivers, water pollution, and river canalization. [1, 2]

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Specialized cornea and twin foveae in the eye compensate for light refraction between air and water. [1]

### Ecological role and importance
Bioindicator of clean, fish-rich freshwater ecosystems. [1]

### Interesting facts
- Its dazzling turquoise feathers contain no blue pigment—the colour comes from microscopic nanostructures scattering light! [1]
- Its streamlined beak inspired the nose cone of Japan's Shinkansen bullet train to eliminate tunnel sonic booms. [1]

### Uncertain or variable information
Global population trend is listed as unknown by IUCN due to natural weather-driven fluctuations.
