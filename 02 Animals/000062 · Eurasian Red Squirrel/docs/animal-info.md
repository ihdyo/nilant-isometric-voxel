## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Arboreal rodent with bright rufous-red coat (varying to dark brown/black in some regions), white underbelly, prominent winter ear tufts, and a bushy tail nearly as long as its body. [1]

### Size and weight
Head-and-body 19–23 cm plus 15–20 cm tail; weight 250–340 g. [1]

### Lifespan
3–7 years in the wild (up to 10 in captivity). [1]

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Seeds of conifers (spruce, pine, larch), nuts, acorns, fungi, shoots, and bark; caches food in tree holes and buried hoards. [1]

### Habitat
Boreal taiga, pine woods, mixed deciduous forests, and wooded parks. [1]

### Geographic distribution and native regions
Across Eurasia from Ireland and Britain through continental Europe and Siberia to Hokkaido and northeastern China. [1]

### Habits and behaviour
Diurnal and strictly arboreal; builds spherical twig nests ('dreys') lined with moss high in tree forks. [1]

### Social structure
Solitary; may share dreys in freezing winter weather for warmth. [1]

### Reproduction
Gestation ~38 days; 1–2 litters per year of 3–4 blind, hairless kits. [1]

### Predators and threats
Habitat fragmentation and competition/squirrelpox virus from introduced eastern grey squirrels in Britain and Italy. [1, 2]

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Double-jointed ankles rotate 180 degrees so it can climb headfirst down vertical tree trunks. [1]

### Ecological role and importance
Vital seed disperser for European and Siberian forests—forgotten caches sprout into new trees. [1]

### Interesting facts
- Its bushy tail acts as a rudder while leaping between branches and a warm blanket wrapped around its body while sleeping. [1]
- In winter it grows dramatic long tufts of fur on the tips of its ears that shed each spring. [1]

### Uncertain or variable information
Coat colour ranges from bright red to nearly black (melanistic) depending on latitude and altitude.
