## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
A heavy bear with a white coat and black legs, shoulder band, ears and eye patches; a short tail; an enlarged wrist bone covered by a pad (the 'panda's thumb') helps it hold bamboo [1].

### Size and weight
Total length 1.5–1.8 m; weight 80–125 kg [1].

### Lifespan
Typically 10–15 years in the wild; up to about 30 years in captivity (34 recorded) [1].

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Bamboo makes up over 99% of the diet; pandas feed 10–12 hours a day and extract only about 17% of the nutrients; they also take bamboo shoots, fruit, small mammals, fish and insects [1].

### Habitat
Montane and mixed coniferous-broadleaf forests with a bamboo understory, 1,200–3,900 m [1].

### Geographic distribution and native regions
Mountains of Sichuan, Gansu and Shaanxi provinces, China; about 5,900 km² of panda habitat within a 29,500 km² range [1].

### Habits and behaviour
Spends most of the day feeding and resting; mostly solitary [1].

### Social structure
Solitary except in the breeding season [1].

### Reproduction
Gestation 112–163 days; 1–3 young (about 1.7 on average); newborns weigh 85–140 g [1]. Notoriously reluctant to breed in captivity [1].

### Predators and threats
Humans are the only known predator today; tigers may have preyed on them in the past [1].

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
The pseudo-thumb (a pad over an enlarged wrist bone) and strong jaws and teeth for crushing bamboo [1].

### Ecological role and importance
A bamboo specialist of central Chinese mountain forests; an emblem of conservation [1][2].

### Interesting facts
- That 'thumb' isn't a finger. It's a pad over a wrist bone, perfect for gripping bamboo. [1]
- A newborn panda weighs just 85 to 140 g. That's lighter than an apple! [1]

### Uncertain or variable information
Conservation status changed: Endangered until 2016, Vulnerable since the 2016 IUCN assessment [2]; the ADW page still says Endangered [1]. Population counts depend on national survey methods [2].
