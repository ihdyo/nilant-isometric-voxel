## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Medium-to-large Arctic deer with dense hollow-haired coat, white neck ruff, furry muzzle, crescent-shaped snowshoe hooves, and complex branching antlers in both sexes. [1]

### Size and weight
Shoulder height 0.9–1.4 m; length 1.6–2.2 m; weight 60–318 kg. [1]

### Lifespan
12–15 years in the wild (up to 20 in captivity). [1]

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Specializes on ground and arboreal lichens (Cladonia) in winter; grazes grasses, herbs, fungi, and shrubs in summer. [1]

### Habitat
Arctic tundra, mountain tundra, and northern taiga forests. [1]

### Geographic distribution and native regions
Circumpolar across Norway, Finland, Russia, Greenland, Alaska, and Canada (known as caribou in North America). [1]

### Habits and behaviour
Undertakes the longest terrestrial migrations of any mammal—up to 5,000 km annually; tendons in feet click while walking. [1]

### Social structure
Forms massive migratory herds numbering tens to hundreds of thousands of individuals. [1]

### Reproduction
Gestation ~228 days; single calf born in May–June that can outrun a human within hours of birth. [1]

### Predators and threats
Climate warming causing rain-on-snow ice crusts that lock away winter lichen, habitat fragmentation, and industrial development. [1, 2]

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Hoof pads shrink and harden in winter to expose sharp rims for gripping ice; nasal turbinates warm freezing inhaled air. [1]

### Ecological role and importance
Primary grazer of Arctic tundra, cycling nutrients and sustaining wolves, bears, and indigenous herding cultures. [1]

### Interesting facts
- The only deer species in which both males and females grow antlers every year. [1]
- Their eye tapetum shifts from gold in summer to deep blue in winter to see ultraviolet light on snow. [1]

### Uncertain or variable information
Wild and semi-domesticated herds share the same species; taxonomy of subspecies (woodland vs barren-ground caribou) is actively revised.
