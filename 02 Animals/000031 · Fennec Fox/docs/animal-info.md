## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Smallest member of the family Canidae, with a cream-fawn coat, black-tipped bushy tail, fur-covered paw soles and enormous ears up to 15 cm long [1].

### Size and weight
Head-and-body length 24–41 cm, tail 18–31 cm, shoulder height about 20 cm, and weight 0.8–1.5 kg [1].

### Lifespan
10–14 years [1].

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Omnivorous desert forager eating insects, scorpions, lizards, gerbils, birds, eggs, tubers and desert fruits [1].

### Habitat
Sand dunes and arid desert regions across the Sahara from Morocco to Egypt and south to Niger and Sudan [2].

### Geographic distribution and native regions
North Africa and the Sahara Desert [2].

### Habits and behaviour
Nocturnal and fossorial, digging extensive multi-entrance burrows up to 1 m deep to escape daytime heat [1].

### Social structure
Lives in small family groups of up to 10 individuals [1].

### Reproduction
Gestation 50–52 days; 2–5 pups born once a year in spring [1].

### Predators and threats
Trapping for the pet trade and tourist display, though widespread across remote desert habitats [2].

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Large vascular ears dissipate body heat and pinpoint prey moving beneath sand; kidneys conserve water so efficiently it rarely needs to drink [1, 3].

### Ecological role and importance
Key nocturnal insectivore and small-vertebrate predator in Saharan dune ecosystems [1].

### Interesting facts
- The smallest wild canid in the world, yet its ears reach 15 cm long to radiate desert heat and hear prey underground. [1,3]
- Thick fur covers even the soles of its paws, protecting them from scorching Sahara sand. [1]
