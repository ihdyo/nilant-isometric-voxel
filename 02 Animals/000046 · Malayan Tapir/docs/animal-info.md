## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Largest living tapir, unmistakable for its jet-black forequarters and hindquarters separated by a broad white midsection saddle, white-tipped ears and a short prehensile proboscis [1].

### Size and weight
Head-and-body length 1.8–2.5 m, tail 5–10 cm, shoulder height 90–110 cm, and weight 250–320 kg [1].

### Lifespan
25–30 years [1].

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Selective browser feeding on tender shoots, leaves, twigs, aquatic vegetation and fallen fruits [1].

### Habitat
Primary and secondary lowland and hill tropical rainforests across Sundaland [1, 2].

### Geographic distribution and native regions
Sumatra (Indonesia), Peninsular Malaysia, southern Thailand and southern Myanmar [2].

### Habits and behaviour
Nocturnal and solitary; follows well-worn jungle trails to rivers and salt licks, swimming well [1, 3].

### Social structure
Solitary outside mating pairs and mother-calf bonds (calves start out dark brown with white spots and stripes) [1].

### Reproduction
Gestation 390–400 days (~13 months); single calf [1].

### Predators and threats
Deforestation for oil palm and agriculture, habitat fragmentation and road collisions [2, 3].

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Disruptive black-and-white coloration camouflages the body in dappled moonlight, while the flexible snout plucks foliage [1].

### Ecological role and importance
Vital long-distance seed disperser for large-seeded rainforest trees [1, 2].

### Interesting facts
- Its bold black-and-white saddle looks striking in daylight, yet breaks up its outline like boulders in moonlit jungle shadows. [1]
- The largest of the four tapir species and the only one native to Asia, closely related to horses and rhinoceroses. [1,2]

### Uncertain or variable information
Total length of 2.3 m reflects typical adult body plus short stubby tail [1].
