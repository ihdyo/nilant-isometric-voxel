## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Large South American camelid with a long neck, thick woolly coat, split upper lip, two-toed padded feet and distinctive inward-curving banana-shaped ears [1].

### Size and weight
Head-and-body length 1.2–2.2 m, tail 20–25 cm, height at head 1.7–1.8 m, and weight 130–200 kg [1].

### Lifespan
15–25 years [1].

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Grazer and browser eating tough Andean grasses, shrubs, herbs and lichens, regurgitating cud like true ruminants [1].

### Habitat
High-altitude Andean Altiplano and mountain pastures (2,300–4,000+ m) [1, 2].

### Geographic distribution and native regions
Domesticated across Peru, Bolivia, Ecuador, Chile and Argentina, with introduced herds worldwide [1, 2].

### Habits and behaviour
Diurnal, social and sure-footed; communicates via humming sounds, ear positions and spitting when agitated [1].

### Social structure
Herd-dwelling animals that form close social bonds and guard livestock against canids [1].

### Reproduction
Gestation ~350 days (11.5 months); single cria able to stand within an hour of birth [1].

### Predators and threats
Domesticated species not evaluated on the IUCN Red List; abundant globally [1, 2].

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
High-concentration elliptical red blood cells bind oxygen efficiently at extreme altitude, while soft foot pads protect fragile mountain soils [1, 3].

### Ecological role and importance
Low-impact high-altitude grazer integral to Andean pastoralism for over four millennia [1, 2].

### Interesting facts
- Domesticated in the Andes over 4,000 years ago from wild guanacos, llamas have banana-shaped inward-curving ears. [1,2]
- Their blood carries extra hemoglobin with high oxygen affinity, letting them thrive above 4,000 meters in thin mountain air. [3]
