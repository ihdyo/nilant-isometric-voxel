## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
The largest rodent: a barrel-shaped body with coarse brown hair, short legs, no visible tail and a large, blunt head with small ears and eyes set high [1].

### Size and weight
Head and body about 106–134 cm; up to 0.6 m at the shoulder; weight 35–66 kg [1].

### Lifespan
About 6 years on average in the wild (up to 10); up to 12 years in captivity [1].

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Mainly grasses and aquatic plants, occasionally bark and fruit; it re-ingests the previous day's food in the morning (coprophagy) [1].

### Habitat
Flooded grasslands, marsh edges and lowland forest with easy access to water; also dry forest, scrub and grassland [1].

### Geographic distribution and native regions
Much of South America east of the Andes, from Venezuela and Colombia south to the Argentine pampas [1].

### Habits and behaviour
A strong swimmer that can stay completely submerged for up to five minutes [1].

### Social structure
Groups of about 10 adults (3–30) [1].

### Reproduction
One litter a year of 2–8 young (about 4) after a 150-day gestation; mature at 18 months [1].

### Predators and threats
Jaguars, green anacondas, spectacled caimans and humans [1].

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Semi-aquatic: water is its refuge from predators [1].

### Ecological role and importance
A major grazer of South American wetlands and prey of large predators [1].

### Interesting facts
- The world's biggest rodent can stay completely underwater for up to five minutes. [1]
- Each morning it re-eats the previous day's food to get more out of it. Gross, but clever! [1]

### Uncertain or variable information
Some local populations decline from over-hunting although the species is Least Concern overall [1][2].
