## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Largest anteater; elongated tubular snout, bold black-and-white diagonal shoulder stripe, knuckle-walking clawed forelimbs, and an enormous bushy tail up to 90 cm long. [1]

### Size and weight
Total length 1.8–2.4 m (tail 65–90 cm); shoulder height ~60 cm; weight 18–41 kg. [1]

### Lifespan
14–16 years in the wild (over 25 in captivity). [1]

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Myrmecophage consuming up to 30,000 ants and termites daily, spending only ~1 minute per mound to avoid soldier bites. [1]

### Habitat
Cerrado savannas, Pantanal wetlands, grasslands, and rainforests. [1]

### Geographic distribution and native regions
Central and South America from Honduras to northern Argentina. [1]

### Habits and behaviour
Terrestrial; diurnal in undisturbed areas and nocturnal near humans; uses its bushy tail as a blanket when sleeping. [1]

### Social structure
Solitary except for mothers carrying a single cub on their backs (whose stripe aligns with the mother's for camouflage). [1]

### Reproduction
Gestation ~190 days; single pup rides on the mother's back for up to 9 months. [1]

### Predators and threats
Habitat loss to soy and cattle agriculture, wildfires in the Cerrado, roadkills, and hunting. [1, 2]

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Sense of smell is 40 times stronger than a human's; lowest body temperature of any placental mammal (~32.7 °C). [1]

### Ecological role and importance
Controls ant and termite populations across South American savannas. [1]

### Interesting facts
- Completely toothless—it crushes insects against hard ridges on the roof of its mouth and muscular stomach. [1]
- It walks on its knuckles with its huge front claws tucked inward so they stay razor-sharp for tearing termite mounds. [1]

### Uncertain or variable information
Extirpated from several Central American countries (including Belize and Guatemala).
