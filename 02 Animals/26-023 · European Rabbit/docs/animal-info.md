## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Greyish coat flecked with black, brown and sometimes red; paler grey below; the underside of the tail white; ears and legs shorter than a hare's [1].

### Size and weight
38–50 cm long, 1.5–2.5 kg [1].

### Lifespan
Up to about 9 years in captivity; in the wild over 90 % die in their first year [1].

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Grasses, leaves, buds, bark, roots and crops; it re-eats some droppings to digest food twice [1].

### Habitat
Dry, low areas with soft sandy soil, brushy fields and woods; also parks and gardens [1].

### Geographic distribution and native regions
Native to the Iberian Peninsula, small parts of France and north-west Africa; now wild on every continent except Asia and Antarctica [1].

### Habits and behaviour
Lives in warrens; mostly active at night and dusk; thumps its hind feet to warn of danger [1].

### Social structure
Colonies of about 6–10 adults with dominance hierarchies [1].

### Reproduction
Gestation about 30 days; litters of 5–6 on average (1–14), born naked and blind; several litters a year [1].

### Predators and threats
Foxes and other carnivores, hawks and owls; myxomatosis cut populations by more than 90 % in places [1].

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Re-eating soft droppings to get the most from its food [1].

### Ecological role and importance
Its burrows shelter other animals; where introduced, as in Australia, it does great damage [1].

### Interesting facts
- It thumps its back feet on the ground to warn the warren that danger is near. [1]
- It's the wild ancestor of every pet rabbit, about 80 breeds in all. [1]

### Uncertain or variable information
The global status (Endangered) is for the native range only; introduced populations are not counted [2].
