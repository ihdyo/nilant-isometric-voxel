## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
North America's largest rodent; stocky waterproof brown body, webbed hind feet, large orange chisel incisors, and a broad flat scaly paddle tail. [1]

### Size and weight
Total length 0.9–1.2 m (tail 25–35 cm long, 13–18 cm wide); weight 11–32 kg. [1]

### Lifespan
10–15 years in the wild (up to 24 in captivity). [1]

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Herbivore eating cambium (inner bark), leaves, and twigs of deciduous trees (aspen, willow, birch) and aquatic macrophytes; caches branches underwater for winter. [1]

### Habitat
Forested rivers, streams, ponds, and wetlands across North America. [1]

### Geographic distribution and native regions
Across Canada, Alaska, the continental United States, and northern Mexico. [1]

### Habits and behaviour
Nocturnal and crepuscular; slaps its flat tail loudly on water to warn family members of danger. [1]

### Social structure
Lives in monogamous family colonies (parents, yearlings, and kits) sharing a stick-and-mud lodge with underwater entrances. [1]

### Reproduction
Gestation ~107 days; single annual litter of 2–4 furred kits born in spring that can swim within 24 hours. [1]

### Predators and threats
Historically trapped nearly to extinction for fur hats; today populations have rebounded strongly. [1, 2]

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Transparent nictitating membranes act as swim goggles; fur-lined lips close behind incisors so it can gnaw wood underwater. [1]

### Ecological role and importance
Keystone species whose dams store groundwater, reduce downstream flooding, filter pollutants, and create biodiversity hotspots. [1]

### Interesting facts
- Nature's master ecosystem engineer—it builds dams of logs and mud that create thriving wetland habitats for hundreds of species. [1]
- Its front teeth are bright orange because their enamel is reinforced with iron, making them self-sharpening. [1]

### Uncertain or variable information
Largest known beaver dam in Wood Buffalo National Park, Canada, stretches 850 metres.
