## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Compact seabird nicknamed 'sea parrot'; black upperparts and neck band, white belly, pale grey cheeks, bright orange webbed feet, and a large triangular grooved orange-and-blue bill. [1]

### Size and weight
Body length 26–30 cm; wingspan 47–63 cm; weight 320–480 g. [1]

### Lifespan
20+ years in the wild (oldest ringed bird lived 36 years). [1]

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Piscivore pursuing small schooling fish (sand eels, sprats, capelin) underwater at depths down to 60 m. [1]

### Habitat
Pelagic North Atlantic Ocean; breeds in soil burrows on grassy coastal cliffs and predator-free islands. [1]

### Geographic distribution and native regions
North Atlantic coasts and islands of Iceland, Norway, Britain, Ireland, Greenland, and eastern Canada. [1]

### Habits and behaviour
Beats wings up to 400 times per minute in rapid whirring flight; swims underwater using wings as flippers. [1]

### Social structure
Highly colonial on breeding cliffs; solitary at sea over winter. [1]

### Reproduction
Monogamous pairs lay a single white egg in a cliff burrow; incubated ~40 days; chick ('puffling') fledges alone at night. [1]

### Predators and threats
Ocean warming shifting sand-eel prey northward, overfishing, marine pollution, and invasive rats/mink on nesting islands. [1, 2]

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Serrated tongue holds multiple fish while catching more; salt glands excrete excess seawater. [1]

### Ecological role and importance
Key indicator of North Atlantic marine food-web health. [1]

### Interesting facts
- Backward-pointing spines on its tongue and palate let it hold up to 60 small fish crosswise in its beak at once! [1]
- Its famous rainbow bill is only worn in summer—in winter it sheds the outer plates and turns smaller and dull grey. [1]

### Uncertain or variable information
Over 60% of the world's Atlantic Puffins breed in Iceland.
