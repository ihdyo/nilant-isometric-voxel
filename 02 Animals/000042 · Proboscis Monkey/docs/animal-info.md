## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Striking colobine monkey with a cinnamon-orange cap and mantle, pale grey limbs and long white tail, a rounded belly and—in adult males—a large pendulous fleshy nose [1].

### Size and weight
Head-and-body length 53–62 cm in females and 66–76 cm in males; tail 55–75 cm; weight 7–12 kg in females and 16–24 kg in males [1].

### Lifespan
15–20 years [1].

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Folivore-frugivore eating young mangrove and riverine leaves, unripe seeds and fruits, avoiding ripe sugary fruit that would disrupt gut fermentation [1].

### Habitat
Coastal mangroves, nipa swamps and lowland riverine forests, rarely venturing more than 1 km from water [1, 2].

### Geographic distribution and native regions
Endemic to the island of Borneo [2].

### Habits and behaviour
Diurnal arboreal climber and exceptional swimmer capable of swimming underwater up to 20 m to evade crocodiles [1].

### Social structure
Lives in one-male, multi-female harem groups of 10–30 that gather at riverside sleeping trees at dusk [1].

### Reproduction
Gestation ~166 days; single dark-faced infant [1].

### Predators and threats
Clearing of coastal mangrove and riverine forests for aquaculture, oil palm and logging [2].

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
A sacculated four-chambered stomach detoxifies leaf alkaloids, while enlarged nasal cavities act as acoustic resonators [1, 3].

### Ecological role and importance
Primary folivore and seed disperser of Borneo's coastal mangrove ecosystems [1, 2].

### Interesting facts
- Adult males grow a fleshy nose over 10 cm long that amplifies honking calls to attract mates and warn rivals. [1,3]
- They are the finest swimmers among all primates, with partially webbed toes and leaps straight into rivers from 15 meters up. [1]

### Uncertain or variable information
