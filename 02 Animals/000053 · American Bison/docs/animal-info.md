## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Massive bovid with a towering muscular shoulder hump, huge woolly head with chin beard, short curved horns, heavy front coat, and sleeker tan hindquarters. [1]

### Size and weight
Shoulder height 1.5–2.0 m; head-and-body length 2.1–3.8 m; weight 318–1,000 kg (record bulls over 1,100 kg). [1]

### Lifespan
15–20 years in the wild (up to 30 in captivity). [1]

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Obligate grazer feeding primarily on warm- and cool-season prairie grasses and sedges. [1]

### Habitat
Temperate grasslands, plains, river valleys, and open woodlands. [1]

### Geographic distribution and native regions
Native to North America; conservation herds live across national parks and reserves in the US and Canada. [1]

### Habits and behaviour
Diurnal grazer that wallows in dust bowls to deter biting insects and shed winter fur. [1]

### Social structure
Cows and calves live in matriarchal herds; mature bulls live alone or in small bachelor bands outside the summer rut. [1]

### Reproduction
Gestation ~285 days; a single reddish-orange calf ('red dog') is born in April–May. [1]

### Predators and threats
Historic near-extinction (reduced from ~30 million to <1,000 in the 1880s); today limited by habitat fragmentation and cattle gene introgression. [1, 2]

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Dense double-layered winter wool insulates so well that snow rests unmelted on its back. [1]

### Ecological role and importance
Keystone prairie engineer whose grazing, trampling, and dust wallows create diverse microhabitats for birds and insects. [1]

### Interesting facts
- North America's heaviest land mammal—despite weighing nearly a tonne, it can sprint at 56 km/h and jump 1.8 metres high. [1]
- Their massive head and shoulder hump act like a snowplow to sweep away deep winter snow from grass. [1]

### Uncertain or variable information
Two subspecies are recognized: Plains bison (B. b. bison) and larger Wood bison (B. b. athabascae).
