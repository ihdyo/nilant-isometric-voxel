## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Named for the greenish colour of its fat, not its shell; the second largest sea turtle [1].

### Size and weight
Adults have a 100–120 cm shell and weigh 150–200 kg; hatchlings are about 5 cm and 25 g [1].

### Lifespan
Up to 75 years recorded (AnAge, via ADW) [1].

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Juveniles eat small invertebrates as well as algae and seagrass; adults are mostly herbivores, grazing seagrass and algae [1].

### Habitat
Shallow tropical and subtropical waters and coastal feeding areas; sandy beaches for nesting [1].

### Geographic distribution and native regions
Tropical and subtropical seas worldwide; nesting on beaches in over 140 countries [1].

### Habits and behaviour
Adults swim about 20–90 km a day; juveniles drift in the open ocean for several years [1].

### Social structure
Mostly solitary outside breeding [1].

### Reproduction
Females return to their home beach every 2–4 years and lay 1–9 clutches a season of 75–200 eggs; nests below 28.5 °C produce more males, above 30.3 °C more females; maturity takes 27–50 years [1].

### Predators and threats
Eggs and hatchlings are eaten by many animals; fishing, artificial light and poaching of eggs and meat [1].

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Temperature decides the sex of the hatchlings [1].

### Ecological role and importance
Grazing adults fertilise seagrass beds and help them regrow [1].

### Interesting facts
- It's named for the green fat under its shell, not for the shell itself! [1]
- Warm nests hatch mostly girls and cool nests mostly boys. [1]

### Uncertain or variable information
The lifespan figure is a recorded maximum, not an average [1].
