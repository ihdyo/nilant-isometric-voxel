## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Dome-backed spiny monotreme covered in 5 cm cream-and-black hollow spines, toothless slender snout, and powerful backward-curving hind claws. [1]

### Size and weight
Length 35–53 cm; weight 2–7 kg (Tasmanian subspecies is larger and hairier). [1]

### Lifespan
Up to 45 years in the wild (over 50 in captivity). [1]

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Insectivore feeding on ants, termites, beetle larvae, and worms using a fast sticky tongue (up to 100 flicks per minute). [1]

### Habitat
Virtually every terrestrial habitat across Australia and New Guinea, from deserts to alpine snowfields. [1]

### Geographic distribution and native regions
Widespread across mainland Australia, Tasmania, Kangaroo Island, and southern/eastern New Guinea. [1]

### Habits and behaviour
Solitary and active day or night depending on temperature; enters torpor in cold weather. [1]

### Social structure
Solitary except during winter breeding 'trains' where up to 10 males follow one female. [1]

### Reproduction
Lays a single grape-sized leathery egg into a temporary pouch; hatches in 10 days; young ('puggle') stays in pouch ~50 days until spines grow. [1]

### Predators and threats
Road vehicles, feral cats, foxes, and dingoes. [1, 2]

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Electroreceptors at the tip of the snout detect insects underground; can survive bushfires by burrowing deep and entering torpor. [1]

### Ecological role and importance
Turns over tonnes of soil per hectare each year while foraging, aiding seed germination and water infiltration. [1]

### Interesting facts
- Like the platypus, it lays a single soft-shelled egg—which it incubates inside a temporary belly pouch! [1]
- When startled, it curls into a spiky ball or digs straight downward into the soil in seconds. [1]

### Uncertain or variable information
Five geographic subspecies are recognized across Australia and New Guinea.
