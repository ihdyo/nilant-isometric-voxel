## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Medium-sized pale owl with unmistakable white heart-shaped facial disc, dark black eyes, golden-ochre wings speckled with grey, and long slender legs. [1]

### Size and weight
Body length 33–39 cm; wingspan 80–95 cm; weight 290–460 g. [1]

### Lifespan
4–10 years in the wild (up to 20 in captivity). [1]

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Small terrestrial mammals—primarily voles, mice, and rats—swallowed whole and indigestible fur/bones regurgitated as glossy black pellets. [1]

### Habitat
Open agricultural landscapes, grasslands, savannas, and woodland edges from sea level to 3,000 m. [1]

### Geographic distribution and native regions
One of the most widespread landbirds on Earth, found on every continent except Antarctica (including Southeast Asia/Indonesia). [1]

### Habits and behaviour
Strictly nocturnal; flies buoyantly and silently low over fields listening for prey. [1]

### Social structure
Solitary or in monogamous pairs roosting in tree cavities, cliffs, barns, or nest boxes. [1]

### Reproduction
Incubation ~30–34 days; 4–7 eggs laid asynchronously so chicks vary in size. [1]

### Predators and threats
Secondary poisoning from anticoagulant rodenticides, loss of old cavity trees, and road collisions. [1, 2]

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Asymmetric ear openings (left higher than right) allow 3D acoustic triangulation of prey. [1]

### Ecological role and importance
Premier natural rodent controller—a single family eats over 1,000 rodents per breeding season. [1]

### Interesting facts
- Its heart-shaped facial disc acts like a satellite dish, funneling faint rustling sounds to asymmetrically placed ears. [1]
- It can pinpoint and catch a mouse hidden completely under snow or grass in pitch darkness. [1]

### Uncertain or variable information
Taxonomists split the barn owl complex into 1–3 species (Tyto alba, T. furcata, T. javanica).
