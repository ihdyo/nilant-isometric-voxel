## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Compact, heavily muscled cat with a tawny-gold coat patterned with large black rosettes enclosing inner spots, a broad head and powerful jaws [1].

### Size and weight
Head-and-body length 1.12–1.85 m, tail 45–75 cm, shoulder height 63–76 cm, and weight 36–158 kg [1].

### Lifespan
12–15 years in the wild and up to 23 years in captivity [1].

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Over 85 prey species recorded, including capybaras, peccaries, deer, tapirs, caimans, river turtles and fish [1].

### Habitat
Dense lowland rainforests, seasonally flooded swamps (such as the Pantanal), mangroves and scrub forests near water [1, 2].

### Geographic distribution and native regions
From northern Mexico through Central America and the Amazon Basin to northern Argentina [2].

### Habits and behaviour
Strong swimmer and climber; often kills large prey by piercing the temporal bones of the skull or turtle shells [1, 3].

### Social structure
Solitary and territorial, communicating via vocal roars, scrapes and scent marks [1].

### Reproduction
Gestation 93–105 days; litters of 1–4 (usually 2) cubs remain with their mother for up to 2 years [1].

### Predators and threats
Deforestation for cattle ranching and agriculture, habitat fragmentation and retaliatory hunting [2].

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Short, robust limbs and massive jaw musculature generate the highest bite force relative to body size among all felids [3].

### Ecological role and importance
Top terrestrial and semi-aquatic predator regulating prey populations across Neotropical ecosystems [1, 2].

### Interesting facts
- The largest cat in the Americas, possessing the strongest bite force relative to body size of any felid. [1,3]
- Unlike most cats, jaguars love water and routinely swim across wide rivers to hunt caimans and turtles. [1]

### Uncertain or variable information
Total length of 2.3 m reflects large Pantanal males combining body and tail ranges [1].
