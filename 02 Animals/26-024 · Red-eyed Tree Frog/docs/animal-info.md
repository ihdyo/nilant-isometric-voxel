## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Leaf-green body, bulging red eyes, webbed orange feet, blue and yellow stripes on the flanks and legs; smooth skin [1].

### Size and weight
4–7 cm, females larger; 6–15 g [1].

### Lifespan
About 5 years in the wild, over 8 reported in captivity [1].

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Insects such as flies, grasshoppers and mosquitoes, and other arthropods, caught at night [1].

### Habitat
Humid forest near ponds and small streams, from sea level to 1,250 m [1].

### Geographic distribution and native regions
Southern Mexico, Central America and northern South America [1].

### Habits and behaviour
Nocturnal and arboreal; flashes its red eyes to startle predators; males croak and shake branches to signal [1].

### Social structure
Seen in groups; no known hierarchy [1].

### Reproduction
Eggs (10–100, about 40) laid on leaves over water in the rainy season; hatch in 4–6 days, earlier under predation; no parental care [1]. Embryos hatch early when snakes or wasps attack the clutch [3].

### Predators and threats
Wasps, snakes, fish, spiders, birds, bats and monkeys at different ages; deforestation and amphibian diseases [1].

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Startle colours and early hatching in response to danger [1][3].

### Ecological role and importance
An insect eater of the rainforest [1].

### Interesting facts
- Unborn tadpoles can hatch early and drop into the water if a snake attacks their eggs! [3]
- It flashes its big red eyes to startle a hungry predator. [1]

### Uncertain or variable information
ADW notes the population is decreasing though still Least Concern [1].
