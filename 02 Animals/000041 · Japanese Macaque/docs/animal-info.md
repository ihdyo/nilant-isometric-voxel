## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Stocky medium-sized macaque with dense grey-brown fur, a bare rosy-red face and rump, cheek pouches for storing food and a short tail under 10 cm [1].

### Size and weight
Head-and-body length 47–60 cm, tail 7.5–10 cm, and weight 8–14 kg [1].

### Lifespan
25–30 years [1].

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Omnivorous feeder consuming fruits, seeds, young leaves, buds, bark, fungi, insects and marine invertebrates [1].

### Habitat
Subtropical to subalpine forests across Japan, enduring winter snows and sub-zero temperatures [1, 2].

### Geographic distribution and native regions
Endemic to Japan (Honshu, Shikoku, Kyushu and Yakushima; absent from Hokkaido) [2].

### Habits and behaviour
Diurnal, semi-terrestrial and famous for soaking in geothermal hot springs in Nagano during winter [1, 3].

### Social structure
Lives in matrilineal troops of 20–100 individuals with stable female dominance hierarchies [1].

### Reproduction
Gestation ~173 days; single infant born in spring [1].

### Predators and threats
Locally managed due to crop raiding, though overall population is large and stable [2].

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Thick winter undercoat and short tail minimize heat loss (Allen's rule), while hot-spring bathing lowers winter stress hormones [1, 3].

### Ecological role and importance
Primary seed disperser and forest browser across Japanese temperate woodlands [1].

### Interesting facts
- Known as 'snow monkeys', they are the northernmost-living non-human primates and famously soak in volcanic hot springs. [1,3]
- Troops pass down learned cultural behaviors, such as washing sweet potatoes in seawater before eating them. [1]
