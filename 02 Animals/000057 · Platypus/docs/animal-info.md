## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Semi-aquatic egg-laying mammal with streamlined dark brown fur, rubbery slate-grey duck-like bill, webbed feet, and a flat paddle tail. [1]

### Size and weight
Total length 43–50 cm (males up to 60 cm); weight 0.7–2.4 kg. [1]

### Lifespan
12–17 years in the wild (over 20 in captivity). [1]

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Carnivorous bottom-feeder consuming benthic invertebrates (caddisfly larvae, freshwater shrimp, worms), storing prey in cheek pouches to crush at the surface with keratin pads. [1]

### Habitat
Permanent freshwater rivers, creeks, and lakes with earth banks suitable for nesting burrows. [1]

### Geographic distribution and native regions
Endemic to eastern mainland Australia (from Queensland to Victoria) and Tasmania. [1]

### Habits and behaviour
Nocturnal and crepuscular swimmer; makes up to 75 dives per hour while foraging. [1]

### Social structure
Solitary outside the spring breeding season. [1]

### Reproduction
Female lays 1–3 leathery eggs in a grass-lined nesting chamber, incubating them for ~10 days; young lap milk secreted from skin patches. [1]

### Predators and threats
Drought, river damming, bank erosion, pollution, and entanglement in illegal yabby traps. [1, 2]

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Bill contains ~40,000 electroreceptors and 60,000 mechanoreceptors; dense fur traps air so skin stays completely dry. [1]

### Ecological role and importance
Apex invertebrate predator in eastern Australian freshwater streams. [1]

### Interesting facts
- One of only five egg-laying mammals (monotremes) on Earth—and males carry a venomous spur on each hind ankle! [1]
- It hunts with eyes, ears and nostrils shut tight, sensing the tiny electrical pulses of prey muscles with its bill. [1]

### Uncertain or variable information
Reclassified from Least Concern to Near Threatened by the IUCN due to increasing drought and river regulation.
