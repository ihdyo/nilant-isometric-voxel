## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Towering deer with dark brown-black fur, pale lower legs, high humped shoulders, a fleshy throat flap (bell), overhanging muzzle, and broad palmate antlers in males. [1]

### Size and weight
Shoulder height 1.4–2.1 m; head-and-body length 2.4–3.1 m; weight 200–700 kg (Alaskan bulls reach 800 kg). [1]

### Lifespan
15–25 years in the wild. [1]

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Browses twigs, bark, and leaves of willow, birch, and aspen; wades into lakes in summer to eat submerged aquatic vegetation. [1]

### Habitat
Boreal taiga forests, wetlands, muskegs, and riparian corridors with seasonal snow cover. [1]

### Geographic distribution and native regions
Circumpolar across Canada, Alaska, northern USA, Scandinavia, the Baltic states, and Russia. [1]

### Habits and behaviour
Mostly solitary outside the autumn rut; active at dawn and dusk; strong swimmer capable of 10 km/h in water. [1]

### Social structure
Solitary adults, though mothers stay with calves for a year until the next calf is born. [1]

### Reproduction
Gestation ~230 days (8 months); 1 or 2 reddish-brown calves born in May–June. [1]

### Predators and threats
Gray wolves, brown bears, winter ticks, and vehicle collisions; locally impacted by warming boreal climates. [1, 2]

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Long legs act like stilts in deep snow and marsh mud; specialized nasal valves close when browsing underwater. [1]

### Ecological role and importance
Shapes boreal forest succession by selective browsing and transfers aquatic nutrients onto land. [1]

### Interesting facts
- The largest living member of the deer family—bulls grow palm-shaped antlers spanning up to 1.8 metres. [1]
- Strong swimmers that can dive up to 5 metres deep to graze on sodium-rich aquatic plants. [1]

### Uncertain or variable information
North American and Eurasian populations are sometimes split into two species (Alces americanus and Alces alces).
