## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Stocky medium-sized mammal with grizzled grey-brown fur, a black facial mask bordered by white, dexterous five-toed front paws and a bushy ringed tail [1].

### Size and weight
Head-and-body length 40–70 cm, tail 20–40 cm, and weight 3.5–9 kg [1].

### Lifespan
2–3 years on average in the wild; up to 20 years in captivity [1].

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Omnivorous forager eating crayfish, frogs, insects, fruits, nuts, grains and bird eggs [1].

### Habitat
Woodlands near water, marshes, mangroves and suburban neighborhoods [1, 2].

### Geographic distribution and native regions
Native from southern Canada through the United States and Mexico to Panama; introduced in parts of Europe and Japan [2].

### Habits and behaviour
Nocturnal and highly dexterous; manipulates objects with sensitive forepaws packed with mechanoreceptors [1, 3].

### Social structure
Mostly solitary, though related females may share overlapping home ranges and winter dens [1].

### Reproduction
Gestation 63–65 days; 2–5 kits born in tree hollows or burrows in spring [1].

### Predators and threats
Expanding and abundant across its range [2].

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Ultra-sensitive forepaw skin becomes even more responsive when wet, explaining its famous dousing behavior [3].

### Ecological role and importance
Versatile omnivore and seed disperser across riparian and forest habitats [1].

### Interesting facts
- Wetting its front paws softens the skin and boosts nerve sensitivity, making 'food washing' a way of feeling food. [3]
- Its black bandit mask reduces glare and enhances night vision during nocturnal foraging. [1]

### Uncertain or variable information
Total length of 0.8 m combines body and ringed tail lengths [1].
