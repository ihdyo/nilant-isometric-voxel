## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Largest living hyena, with a coarse sandy-yellow coat marked with dark spots, high muscular shoulders sloping down to shorter hindquarters, rounded ears and massive jaws [1].

### Size and weight
Head-and-body length 95–165.8 cm, tail 25–36 cm, shoulder height 70–91.5 cm, and weight 40–86 kg (females heavier than males) [1].

### Lifespan
12–25 years in the wild (up to 41 years in captivity) [1].

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Skilled pack hunters taking wildebeest, zebras, gazelles, impalas and warthogs, as well as scavenging carrion and digesting bones [1].

### Habitat
Savannas, grasslands, open woodlands and semi-arid plains across sub-Saharan Africa [1, 2].

### Geographic distribution and native regions
Widespread across sub-Saharan Africa, especially East and Southern Africa [2].

### Habits and behaviour
Active at night and twilight; communicates with over a dozen distinct vocalizations including whoops and the famous 'giggle' [1, 3].

### Social structure
Lives in complex fission-fusion clans of up to 80 individuals governed by a strict female dominance hierarchy [1, 3].

### Reproduction
Gestation ~110 days; 1–2 cubs born in communal dens with eyes open and teeth erupted [1].

### Predators and threats
Persecution outside protected areas, poisoning and habitat loss [2].

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Conical premolars and vaulted skull resist extreme forces to crack open marrow-rich ungulate bones [1].

### Ecological role and importance
Africa's most abundant large carnivore, vital for both prey regulation and carcass cleanup [1, 2].

### Interesting facts
- Spotted hyena clans are strict matriarchies where every female outranks every adult male. [1,3]
- Their bone-crushing jaws exert over 4,500 newtons of force, letting them digest bones, teeth and hooves. [1]

### Uncertain or variable information
Total nose-to-tail length of 1.6 m combines body and tail averages [1].
