## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Largest living primate, characterized by a robust jet-black coat, long muscular arms, heavy brow ridge, wide flared nostrils and a silver-white back saddle in adult males [1].

### Size and weight
Standing height 1.4–1.8 m, arm span 2.0–2.6 m, and weight 70–90 kg in females and 140–200 kg in wild males [1].

### Lifespan
35–40 years in the wild and over 50 years in human care [1].

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Herbivorous/frugivorous diet of leaves, shoots, pith, stems, bark, seasonal fruits and insects [1].

### Habitat
Lowland tropical rainforests, secondary forests and swamp clearings (bais) in western-central Africa [1, 2].

### Geographic distribution and native regions
Cameroon, Central African Republic, Republic of the Congo, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Angola (Cabinda) and Nigeria [2].

### Habits and behaviour
Diurnal terrestrial knuckle-walker that builds ground or low-tree sleeping nests each dusk [1].

### Social structure
Lives in cohesive family troops of 5–20 individuals led by a single dominant silverback male [1].

### Reproduction
Gestation ~255 days; single infant carried by its mother for the first several years [1].

### Predators and threats
Commercial bushmeat poaching, Ebola virus outbreaks, logging and habitat fragmentation [2].

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Enlarged gut volume ferments fibrous plant matter efficiently, while chest-beating communicates male size without risky combat [1, 3].

### Ecological role and importance
Major seed disperser and vegetation gap-creator in Central African forests [1, 2].

### Interesting facts
- The largest living primate; adult males develop a saddle of silver-white hair across their backs around age 12. [1]
- A silverback's chest-beat echoes over 1 km through dense jungle and honestly signals his body size to rivals. [3]

### Uncertain or variable information
Standing height of 1.7 m reflects typical adult silverback stature [1].
