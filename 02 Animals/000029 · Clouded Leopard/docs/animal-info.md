## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Medium-sized wild cat with dusky grey-ochre fur marked by large irregular cloud-shaped blotches, short legs, broad paws and a tail nearly as long as its body [1].

### Size and weight
Head-and-body length 68.6–108 cm, tail 61–91 cm, and weight 11–23 kg [1].

### Lifespan
11–17 years (averaging around 13 years in human care) [1].

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Hunts arboreal and terrestrial prey including primates, squirrels, porcupines, hog deer, wild pigs and birds [1].

### Habitat
Primary tropical evergreen rainforests and mixed deciduous forests from Nepal and northeastern India across mainland Southeast Asia and southern China [2].

### Geographic distribution and native regions
Mainland Southeast Asia, southern China, northeastern India, Nepal and Bhutan [2].

### Habits and behaviour
Remarkably arboreal—capable of climbing down vertical trunks headfirst and hanging by its hind feet [1].

### Social structure
Solitary and elusive; rarely observed directly in the wild [1, 2].

### Reproduction
Gestation 85–93 days; litters of 1–5 (typically 2) cubs [1].

### Predators and threats
Rapid deforestation across Southeast Asia, snaring and illegal wildlife trade [2].

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Flexible ankle joints and a long counterweight tail enable extraordinary tree-canopy agility, while long upper canines resemble sabertooth proportions [1, 3].

### Ecological role and importance
Mid-to-apex forest predator linking arboreal and terrestrial food webs [1].

### Interesting facts
- Rotating hind ankles let it climb down tree trunks headfirst and hang upside down beneath branches. [1]
- It has the longest upper canine teeth relative to skull size of any living wild cat—up to 4 cm long. [3]

### Uncertain or variable information
