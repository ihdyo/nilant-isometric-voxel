## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
A stout body with a thick, mostly white coat; thin black horns 20–30 cm long in both sexes, never shed and growing in yearly rings; large oval hooves with a rubber-like sole [1].

### Size and weight
1.25–1.79 m long; males 61–82 kg, females 57–71 kg [1].

### Lifespan
12–15 years in the wild, 16–20 in captivity [1].

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Grasses, woody plants, mosses, lichens and herbs; most of its water comes from food and snowbanks; travels far to mineral licks [1].

### Habitat
Steep, rocky cliffs and bluffs in alpine and subalpine areas, 1,000–5,000 m [1].

### Geographic distribution and native regions
South-east Alaska to Washington, western Montana and central Idaho; introduced elsewhere [1].

### Habits and behaviour
Climbs steep rock; digs shallow beds for rest and dust baths; moves between winter and summer ranges [1].

### Social structure
Large groups in winter, small groups or alone in summer; adult females dominate most of the year [1].

### Reproduction
Gestation about 147–186 days; 1–3 kids born on very steep cliffs, walking soon after birth [1].

### Predators and threats
Cougars above all, also wolves, bears, golden eagles (taking newborns), coyotes and bobcats [1].

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Rubber-like hoof soles for gripping rock [1].

### Ecological role and importance
A grazer of alpine plants [1].

### Interesting facts
- Its hooves have rubbery soles that grip the rock like climbing shoes. [1]
- Count the rings on its horns to tell its age, just like a tree! [1]

### Uncertain or variable information
ADW gives two breeding seasons (late October to November, or late November to early January) [1].
