## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Largest of the five rhinoceros species, with a massive barrel body, pronounced muscular neck hump, wide square mouth, thick grey skin and two keratin horns [1].

### Size and weight
Head-and-body length 3.4–4.2 m, tail 50–70 cm, shoulder height 1.5–1.85 m, and weight 1,400–3,600 kg [1].

### Lifespan
40–50 years [1].

### Diet and feeding behaviour
Pure grazer feeding almost exclusively on short, palatable savanna grasses [1, 3].

### Habitat
Tropical and subtropical grasslands, savannas and bushveld near permanent water and mud wallows [1, 2].

### Geographic distribution and native regions
Southern white rhino in South Africa, Namibia, Zimbabwe, Botswana, Kenya, Uganda and Eswatini; northern white rhino functionally extinct in the wild [2].

### Habits and behaviour
Diurnal and crepuscular; wallows in mud daily to cool down and deter biting insects [1].

### Social structure
Females and subadults form small herds of up to 14; adult bulls are solitary and territorial, marking boundaries with dung middens [1].

### Reproduction
Gestation ~16 months (480–540 days); single calf weighing 40–65 kg [1].

### Predators and threats
Illegal poaching for rhino horn and habitat loss [2].

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
A wide, flat, prehensile-free upper lip functions like a lawnmower, supported by enlarged neck muscles to lift the heavy head [1, 3].

### Ecological role and importance
Megaherbivore ecosystem engineer that creates 'grazing lawns' benefiting zebras, wildebeest and warthogs [3].

### Interesting facts
- The second-largest land mammal after the elephant; its name comes from Dutch 'wijd' ('wide'), referring to its square grazing lip. [1]
- Unlike African elephants, white rhino calves run in front of their mothers rather than behind them. [1]

### Uncertain or variable information
Total length of 4.2 m reflects large adult bull proportions [1].
