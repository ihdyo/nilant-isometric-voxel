## Biological characteristics

### Physical characteristics
Wide stripes, vertical on the neck and forelegs and wider and more horizontal on the flanks and rump, meeting on the belly; brownish 'shadow' stripes in some populations; a short upright mane [1].

### Size and weight
Head and body 2.17–2.46 m, tail 0.47–0.56 m, 1.1–1.45 m at the shoulder, 175–385 kg [1].

### Lifespan
Up to 40 years in captivity; about 9 on average in the wild [1].

### Diet and feeding behaviour
About 90 % short grass stems and sheaths, with some herbs, leaves and twigs [1].

### Habitat
Open savanna, grassland, woodland and scrub up to 4,400 m [1].

### Geographic distribution and native regions
South-eastern Africa, from southern Ethiopia and Sudan to Namibia and northern South Africa, densest in the Serengeti-Mara [1].

### Habits and behaviour
Walks up to 13 km a day and 100–150 km between seasons; harems join into great herds on migration [1].

### Social structure
Harems of one stallion, 1–6 mares and their young; bachelor groups up to 16 [1].

### Reproduction
Gestation 360–396 days; one foal of about 32 kg, standing within 10–15 minutes [1].

### Predators and threats
Lions, hyenas, wild dogs, cheetahs, leopards and crocodiles; habitat loss and illegal hunting [1].

## Additional information

### Unique adaptations
Stripes: horse flies approach zebras as often as horses but fail to land on them far more often [3].

### Ecological role and importance
A grazer that shares the savanna with wildebeest [1].

### Interesting facts
- Biting flies struggle to land on stripes, so zebras get bitten less than horses! [3]
- No two zebras have the same stripes, just like fingerprints. [1]

### Uncertain or variable information
Why zebras have stripes is still debated; fly protection is the best-supported idea [3]. ADW's wild lifespan figures disagree (9 and 20 years) [1].
